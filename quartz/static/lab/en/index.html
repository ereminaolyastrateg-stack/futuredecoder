Approach

Life as a system.
Built to last — and flexible.

Most personal strategy programmes start from the inside: your drivers, your calling, your value axes. We start from the outside — from the many ways the world already offers what matters to you.

01 / Outside in

Values have external ways

Security doesn't come only from money: services, big systems, your own tools and people nearby give it too. First we look at these ways across the four logics of the world and real signals — then you choose yours.

02 / The whole picture

One map instead of a list of goals

Eight spheres, your “now” and “want”, values and levers — on one scheme. You see which step moves several spheres at once, what you pay for it and what isn't supported yet.

03 / Contextual strategising

Built to last, but not frozen

Not a ten-year plan that breaks at the first change. A system where, when the context changes, the lever changes — not your whole life: testing against different scenarios, review in cycles, the first step as an experiment.

You are already choosing

Work, study, moving — every decision holds an idea of what comes next. In the lab we make these assumptions explicit: we look at options and test the grounds for the choice.

2002Prize to Daniel Kahneman*

Wording changes the choice.

Kahneman and Tversky showed that different descriptions of the same problem can lead to different decisions. It's not only about the answer but about how the question is put.

Tversky and Kahneman · 1981 study ↗
The story of one experiment

Participants were offered options with identical outcomes, described either as lives saved or lives lost. Preferences changed with the wording.

In 2002 Daniel Kahneman received the prize for bringing psychological research into economics, especially the study of decisions under uncertainty. It is not a prize “for designing life”.

01

Information

What do I build my picture of the world from?

How it often goes

I see fragments

News, familiar opinions, alarming events. I may take a visible part for the whole picture.

How it could be

I widen the context

I study different spheres, data and people's experience. I weigh threats, opportunities and what I don't know yet.

Research basis / 01

On average, negative news triggers a stronger physiological response.

Soroka, Fournier, Nir · 2019Learn more

International experimental study

What was studied

In 17 countries, people's physiological responses to real TV news were measured.

What was found

On average the response to negative news was stronger. But people differed considerably.

How we apply it

We don't stop at the news feed. We compare sources and look at changes in work, education, technology and everyday life.

The study doesn't prove that bad news inevitably creates a negative future. Widening the context is our practical principle.

Study: original source ↗
02

Vision of the future

What future do I imagine?

How it often goes

I extend the familiar

I build one image of the future from past experience. Other options may simply not come to mind.

How it could be

I build scenarios

I imagine several options. I separate what I want, what is possible and what I'm only assuming.

Research basis / 02

Memory and imagining the future rely on partly shared brain systems.

Schacter, Addis, Buckner · 2007Learn more

Review of neurocognitive research

What was studied

Data on remembering the past and mentally simulating future events were compared.

What was found

Stored information serves as material for constructing a possible future.

How we apply it

We add new examples to personal experience. In groups we compare different ways to work, study, live and interact.

Imagining a scenario is not predicting an event. Rich imagery doesn't guarantee an accurate forecast.

Study: original source ↗
03

Path

How do I move from an image to action?

How it often goes

I follow the familiar track

I picture the result and start with familiar actions. I don't always check the conditions, obstacles and the cost of the route.

How it could be

I test the route

I weigh the goal against resources and constraints. I choose a small step and the signs by which I'll judge the result.

Research basis / 03

It helps to contrast the desired future with obstacles, not just picture it.

Gabriele Oettingen · 2012Learn more

Review of research on motivation and behaviour

What was studied

How different ways of thinking about a desired future relate to effort and goal attainment.

What was found

Contrasting the future with reality helps match effort to how achievable a goal is — including letting go of unrealistic goals.

How we apply it

We describe the desired transition, constraints and available resources. Then we choose an action that can be tested.

This is not a guarantee of reaching the goal. Sometimes the well-founded result is to change the goal or drop the chosen route.

Study: original source ↗
04

Cognitive
blinkers

What do I stop noticing?

How it often goes

I defend my picture

I look for confirmation of the path I've already chosen. I may skip contradicting information or explain it in my favour.

How it could be

I leave room for the new

I ask: what could disprove my idea? What changes would require rethinking the route?

Research basis / 04

We tend to seek and interpret information in favour of existing beliefs.

Raymond Nickerson · 1998Learn more

Review of research on confirmation bias

What was studied

Data on how people seek and interpret information when they already hold a belief or hypothesis.

What was found

Confirmation bias shows up across tasks and can sustain a one-sided assessment of information.

How we apply it

We formulate alternative explanations and decide in advance which results would make us change the decision.

“Cognitive blinkers” is our metaphor. It isn't a scientific term and doesn't mean the brain literally blocks information to reach a goal.

Study: original source ↗
05

Creativity

What new can I put together along the way?

How it often goes

I repeat what's ready-made

I choose from familiar solutions or copy the visible part of someone else's success. New combinations stay out of the frame.

How it could be

I create my own option

I connect ideas from different spheres. I invent, try and change the solution — not only before the start but along the way.

Research basis / 05

Memories, images of the future and new ideas engage partly shared brain areas.

Roger Beaty and colleagues · 2018Learn more

Functional MRI study

What was studied

Brain activity was compared while participants remembered the past, imagined the future and came up with unusual uses for objects.

What was found

Overlapping activity was found in areas linked to memory, imagining the future and creative thinking.

How we apply it

We work with combinations: one need — different ways to meet it. We don't look for a single “right” way of life.

Overlapping brain activity doesn't prove that the programme boosts creativity. Creativity is possible at any stage, not only the last one.

Study: original source ↗

The five stages are Future Decoder's own scheme, not an established sequence of how the brain works. In life these processes overlap: we refine information, change the route and invent new things at any step.

Each stage has a conclusion, the limits of the research and how we apply it.

04 / A Future Decoder tool

One need.
Different ways.

We look at life through four models of how the world is organised. We don't choose the “right world” — we look for a combination of solutions that fits your conditions.

When it feels “unrealistic, no money, no time”, we ask four questions: what can you hand to others, how can you do without it, what small step already gives a little, who can help. Each answer is already an option for action.

Apply to the eight spheres of life ↗
Centre + Earth

An educational institution

A common curriculum, teachers, a timetable and an in-person community.

Centre + Synthetic

An educational platform

A single digital system, a large catalogue and automated support.

Decentre + Earth

A circle of practitioners

Learn from people, exchange experience, do a joint project.

Decentre + Synthetic

Your own trajectory

Assemble learning from open materials, digital tools and connections.

Centre / decentre — how decisions are organised and distributed. Earth / synthetic — emphasis on living environments or technological systems. These are our own models, not an exhaustive list of ways to live.

05 / From inquiry — to action

Five meetings.
Down to a step — and back up to the system.

Online. 3, 10, 17, 24 January.
The fifth — 7 February, after two weeks of experiment.
Each meeting is 2.5 hours. Six weeks in total.

Meeting 01 / 3 January / 2.5 hours

How is my life arranged now — and what do I want?

At the meeting

Introduction: eight spheres of life and four logics of the world. On a training hero we learn to describe what is and to separate our own wants from imposed ones.

Between meetings

In the online lab you describe “now” in points, mark your wants, what each is for, and which are goals in themselves and which are ways.

Bounded rationality is a reason to make the available options and conditions of choice explicit.

Herbert Simon · 1978 ↗
Meeting 02 / 10 January / 2.5 hours

What other options are possible?

At the meeting

We see how four different worlds meet the same need. Examples from Decoder games. We live a day of the training hero in the life they want.

Between meetings

You live your own Day B, drop wants that aren't yours, and choose the value you'll start with.

Futures Literacy — working with how ideas about the future shape our actions.

UNESCO · Futures Literacy ↗
Meeting 03 / 17 January / 2.5 hours

What holds me back?

At the meeting

We untangle beliefs and clouds — contradictions like “I want money but don't want to work more”. Four “how else” questions: hand it to others, without it, a small step, through people. A lack of resources is also a cloud, and it can be untangled.

Between meetings

You build a field of levers — actions, rules and new pictures — and choose up to three.

We contrast what we want with real obstacles. Clouds follow Eliyahu Goldratt's “thinking processes”: a contradiction rests on an assumption that can be tested.

Gabriele Oettingen · 2012 ↗
Meeting 04 / 24 January / 2.5 hours

What to change so the system works differently?

At the meeting

We build in the levers: what each changes across eight spheres, which loops it starts, what it hurts. A year map by quarters. We choose the first step.

Between meetings

Two weeks of experiment: “when…, I…”, and in advance — what could derail it and how you'll recognise the result.

Leverage points are a tool for finding changes in a system's links, rules and goals. The “when…, I…” plan follows Peter Gollwitzer.

Donella Meadows · Systems analysis ↗
Meeting 05 / 7 February / 2.5 hours

What changed in reality?

At the meeting

We compare intentions, actions and results. We check whether the step would hold if the world swung the other way.

After the lab

You update the map. You decide: continue, change the way or rethink the goal. A new circle starts with “now”.

Our own learning practice: we record observations and revise assumptions. A personal trial is not a scientific experiment.

First we work through each tool on a training hero and in groups. Between meetings you apply it to your own life in the online lab. Personal maps are never discussed in public.

About the project and author ↗
“Not every successful decision improves life as a whole.”

I studied, launched projects, changed ways of working. And gradually I came to a question: how do you choose not a single improvement but a combination of decisions you want to live with?

How I came to this

Out of constraints

I loved maths but believed that without good English many universities were closed to me. I looked for places where it wasn't required. Other options never entered my field of view.

Out of other people's models

In successful people I noticed expensive shoes, but not the whole system of their lives. Copying the visible part wasn't enough.

Out of scattered actions

Professions, study, my own projects. Each decision had its advantages. But success in one sphere didn't necessarily improve life as a whole.

A research approach and my daughter's drawings sit side by side. Rigour helps test ideas. Imagination keeps us from settling for ready-made answers.
