*[[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy|Education, Knowledge and Literacy]] is a macrotrend about who leads the learning path and who recognises the result when knowledge is open to all. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-synthetic|Centralized — Synthetic]]** world, where a platform builds the path and a digital record recognises the result.*

## 2035. Mira, thirteen

In the morning the AI tutor adjusts Mira's maths lesson to yesterday's mistakes. It will explain as many times as needed, and nobody will see that she didn't get it the first time. Every answer goes into her digital record. This year the system recommended an engineering track for her; her mum asked whether it could be changed — it can, but the points in creative subjects can no longer be caught up. Her friend Liza found a way to collect points faster: take short modules where everything is easy. Their teacher, Irina Pavlovna, watches the class dashboard, while the AI grades the essays for her, and she hardly reads the texts themselves.

## Where we come from

Each wave — the university, the book, mass schooling, the internet — made knowledge more accessible while leaving recognition to institutions. Now AI explains anything: 95% of UK students already use it in their studies. What is scarce is no longer knowledge but understanding and recognition. The CS answer: the system takes all of this on — it leads the path, measures progress and keeps the record. [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

A shared system organises access to knowledge, the sequence of learning and assessment. The person gets help, content and a convenient interface; a recommendation suggests what to study next. The original image of this world is the **"digital consumer"**: standardised content on global platforms.

But the CS world is broader than content consumption. What matters most is a person's digital record: verifiable achievements, badges, skill records and a forecast of the next step. A personal offer remains the centre's decision: the goals, criteria and data are not set by you.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** You can learn at your own pace, with endlessly patient explanation, anywhere. You don't have to build the route yourself — the system will pick it. Achievements are portable and verifiable: they are visible beyond a single school.

**What it costs.** It is easy to start learning for points rather than understanding. An early recommendation can close paths that are hard to reopen later. Everything you do is recorded, and it isn't clear who will see it. And a teacher who hands assessment to a machine stops reading your work.

## What the games found

If in CS the system leads the path and a digital record recognises the result, the games explored what exactly it measures and where that leads.

### An assessment changes meaning depending on the decision

The same score can help a student find a gap, allocate places, promote an employee or evaluate a school. So before any metric you need to name who uses it and what follows for the person. In the debate about exams both sides held: a shared exam makes results comparable, and preparing for it sometimes leads to real understanding. Or you can learn only for the format of the task. Participants suggested checking a skill directly, through a practical task, rather than through a description of experience.

> If you don't understand, you won't pass. You just won't pass.

> So assess the skills straight away, rather than looking at what you did before and how artificial intelligence described your experience

<!-- Q-MT031-001, Q-MT031-009, translated from Russian. Sources: first game, line 192; second game, line 226. -->

### A world of process for the sake of process

If the system counts actions, people pile up courses, points and activity while the problem stays unsolved. The amount completed shows activity, not depth or usefulness. At the same time an intermediate result doesn't have to be money: understanding or a new explanation is a result too. Participants suggested asking of every stage what it serves.

> if what matters is assessing the process, and the more activity we stir up around ourselves the higher our score, then it turns out this is some world of process for the sake of process, right?

<!-- Q-MT031-015, translated from Russian. Source: second game, line 203 (facilitator). -->

### A digital record: recognition and surveillance

Around [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-008|Comprehensive Learner Record]] people discussed experience that never reaches a CV: self-study, everyday tasks. Such a record shows a person more fully. But once the process becomes visible, the system starts wanting more and more: mistakes, state of mind, habits. Open questions remained: is disclosure voluntary, can an error in the record be corrected, and can you opt out?

### An early recommendation can close doors

Around [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-014|The 12-Year-Old’s Birthday Party]] people discussed what it means to assign a path in childhood. The risk is that a recommendation is hard to revise, and switching tracks is expensive or formally impossible. A system can offer many options, none of which is actually available to a particular person. There is a strong side too: finding a suitable route faster and reducing a teacher's personal bias. But being automatic doesn't prove being impartial, so the right to switch and to appeal matters.

## What you can do now

1. For any assessment, name who uses it and what decision follows.
2. Check understanding with a practical task and an explanation in one's own words.
3. Remove metrics that count activity but don't help solve the problem.
4. Find out what data is collected about you, who sees it and how to correct an error.
5. Learn how to challenge the system's recommendation and switch to another path.
