*[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy|Transformational Economy]] is a macrotrend about how the idea of value changes when growth is no longer the norm. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|Decentralized — Earth]]** world, where value is confirmed by the close circle — through exchange, gifts and mutual help.*

## 2035. Meat for puppies

Vera's village has no money for machinery, but it has cows. A nearby dog-training centre teaches her two puppies to herd the cattle, and Vera gives them meat. No contracts — just a word and a shared chat for thirty households. The same chat decides who drives the children to town on Saturday, who fixes the line after a storm and who has a generator to lend. In the evening her daughter Sonya comes from the city: she works in a studio that makes furniture to order and sells it only through people she knows — "it feels safer". Sonya says a friend has stopped posting her work online and shows it only to people she knows. Vera nods: in the village it has always been like that.

## Where we come from

Before money and markets, value was confirmed by exchange, debt and gifts inside the community. Markets and the state pushed these mechanisms aside but did not remove them. When growth stops being a promise and the system cannot keep up, people return to the economy of the close circle. [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In DE, value is what your own people confirm: neighbours, friends, community. Exchange, sharing, mutual help and gifts all work. The measure is not growth but a "sufficient life": is there enough for the essentials, and is there someone to turn to. Trust is valued above reach, and people increasingly show what is theirs only to those they know.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Trust and support without money. You can do without what you cannot afford if you come to an agreement with the people around you. Life is simpler and calmer.

**What it costs.** A narrow circle. It is hard for an outsider to get in, there is no scale, and agreements rest on people rather than rules.

## What you can do now

1. Map your close circle: who can do what, who has what, who needs what.
2. Try one exchange without money: a skill for a skill, a thing for help.
3. Start a shared pool of things with neighbours or colleagues: tools, children's things, books.
4. Agree how new people join the circle, so that trust doesn't turn into a closed club.

## Transformational Economy in other worlds

The worlds differ in who decides what things are worth:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-cs|CS]] — the algorithm.** Price, rating and asset value.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-ce|CE]] — the system.** What counts as enough and what to protect.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-ds|DS]] — you yourself.** Your hand, your name and a direct link to the buyer.
- **DE — your own people.** Exchange, gifts and a life that is "enough".
