*[[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy|Education, Knowledge and Literacy]] is a macrotrend about who leads the learning path and who recognises the result when knowledge is open to all. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|Centralized — Earth]]** world, where a school with a big mission teaches and the community recognises.*

## 2035. First year

Gleb got into a university school attached to a national climate and energy programme: forty applicants per place. The first year is the same for everyone — logic, history of science, writing, teamwork — and only then comes specialisation. Every student has a mentor among the programme's researchers; Gleb got an oceanographer who asks more than he explains. The programme pays for his studies, and in return Gleb will work for five years wherever he is sent. His older brother took short courses and is already earning, and at family dinners they argue about which of them made the right choice.

## Where we come from

Each wave — the university, the book, mass schooling, the internet — made knowledge more accessible while leaving recognition to institutions. Now AI explains anything, and what is scarce is no longer knowledge but understanding and recognition. The CE answer: restore the value of a long school with a mentor and a shared purpose, where what is recognised is participation in the cause, not points. [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

An institution, school or recognised community sets a shared direction: what is worth learning, what counts as mastery and why training matters. A person's contribution lies in long participation, practice and the ability to meet high standards. The original image of this world is the **"apprentice and bearer of tradition"**: learning in strong institutions where the diploma belongs to the school but requires your deep involvement.

Signals widen the image of the elite school. A shared mission can bring together local workshops, cultural venues, regional programmes and adult learning. The institution keeps the right of recognition, so access to training and the right to switch paths matter most.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Depth that short courses can't give. A shared language for thinking and agreeing with others. A mentor and an environment stronger than you. Meaning: you learn not just for yourself but for a big cause.

**What it costs.** You don't set the goal, and your place in the cause may be assigned. The path is long and expensive, and hard to leave. And a school's name sometimes weighs more than what you can actually do.

## What the games found

If in CE a school with a mission teaches and the community recognises, the games explored what shared training gives and where its limits are.

### Shared training gives a language

Education in CE was linked to a shared intellectual language: logic, the ability to argue and understand each other. Participants objected to the opposition "useful to the system or useful to the person": the same training can give both. Shared knowledge helps people cooperate, criticise and choose for themselves. But no universal list of knowledge for everyone was drawn up.

### Learning, proving and licensing are different things

An institution's prestige, a mastered skill and the right to do a particular job are three different checks. A diploma can be a filter in hiring but doesn't show practical skill. The reverse happens too: someone can do the work but lacks the required document. [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-016|Solar Mamas]] helped discuss how to recognise the mastery of people whom usual requirements would screen out.

### A mentor: supporting a choice or fixing a role

One hypothesis featured a lifelong mentor who knows the person and helps choose study and work. Such a mentor can support your goal — or prepare you from childhood for a role the system needs. The difference lies in the mentor's powers and your right to change path. The cost was noted too: formally everyone has support, but a good mentor isn't available to all.

### The right to change path depends on resources

Cost, number of places, recognition of previous learning and the price of coming back determine how free a choice really is. Turning down university was explained in different ways: pressure and closed doors — or one's own decision. You can't guess a person's motive from their type of education. The model of "an executing majority and a creative frontier" was discussed as a literary image, not an innate division of people.

## What you can do now

1. Find out what training and licence your chosen field needs, and which requirements belong only to a particular institution.
2. Compare paths by content, cost, recognition and the chance to change direction, not by prestige.
3. Find an environment stronger than you and name what exactly you want to learn there.
4. Check whether support helps your own choice and how to revise an assigned path.
5. Before a big commitment, write down what you want, your resources and your limits.
