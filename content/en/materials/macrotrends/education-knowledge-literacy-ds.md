*[[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy|Education, Knowledge and Literacy]] is a macrotrend about who leads the learning path and who recognises the result when knowledge is open to all. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-synthetic|Decentralized — Synthetic]]** world, where people build their own learning path.*

## 2035. Anya retrains

Anya is thirty-eight, an accountant, and for the second year she has been retraining as an interface designer on her own. She built the route herself: open courses, an AI tutor trained on her own notes, and a study circle at the library on Thursdays, run by another beginner. Instead of a diploma she has a portfolio and a skills passport with digital badges; her first client looked only at the work. She studies in the evenings after work, subscriptions eat a noticeable share of her money, and there are weeks when she wants to quit more than to continue. A year ago the service that held her notes shut down, and since then Anya keeps everything on her own devices.

## Where we come from

Each wave — the university, the book, mass schooling, the internet — made knowledge more accessible while leaving recognition to institutions. Now AI explains anything, and what is scarce is no longer knowledge but understanding and recognition. The DS answer: the person keeps their learning route and the evidence of their skills themselves, independent of institutions. [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

The person chooses the goal, tools and sources, sets the order of work and the ways to see progress. The path combines open materials, digital assistants, their own projects and a chosen community. The original image of this world is the **"techno-sovereign"**: you own your knowledge and learning tools independently of institutions.

The games clarified the conditions of this independence. A tool depends on infrastructure, access to knowledge requires resources, and where others depend on you, external recognition is still needed. Independence doesn't mean loneliness: chosen mentors, rhythm and feedback support it.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** You learn what you need, at your own pace and in your own way. Skills and evidence move with you from place to place. You can change path without starting from scratch.

**What it costs.** Nobody hands out time, money and motivation. Freedom from institutions turns into dependence on platforms and services. Recognition isn't guaranteed: a portfolio still has to be shown and has to convince someone.

## What the games found

If in DS you build your learning path yourself, the games explored how it actually works and what it costs.

### "Knowing how to learn" is a set of concrete actions

Independence was linked to choosing the task, sources, pace and way of checking. Participants proposed breaking "knowing how to learn" into steps: find material, understand it, try to apply it, notice a gap and ask for the needed explanation. The goal sets direction while the route can change. But to get through the hard part, the path has to be stable enough.

### A convenient tool is a new dependence

With a corporate AI you can turn to colleagues less often. But what happens if the provider switches the service off? Working faster and being independent are different things. Your own model, your own archive and working offline change the kind of dependence but require devices, skills and support. Leaving an institution can also close certain access, and that is part of the choice not to hide behind the word "sovereignty".

### You need to be able to explain your own result

When AI is available, a finished answer says little about one's own understanding. Participants suggested explaining material in your own words and checking the answer against the task. In one discussion AI inflated a description of experience, and the wording had to be brought back to what was actually done. A portfolio becomes convincing when it shows the task, the way it was solved and the limits of what you can do.

### The route has a price

Self-directed learning needs time, money, energy, attention and available help. A lack of these can't be fixed by wanting it harder. Participants suggested dropping activities unrelated to the current task and not turning learning into an endless accumulation of courses that crowds out application and rest. Pausing and reconsidering the load are part of the choice too.

## What you can do now

1. Choose your task and break "knowing how to learn" into the actions it needs.
2. Make a small attempt, find an explanation and compare approaches.
3. Keep your materials and work on your own devices, and check whether you could continue without your current service.
4. Check descriptions of your skills, especially ones written with AI, against what you actually did.
5. Match the load to your time, money and energy, and drop unnecessary courses.
