*People act on the images of the future they carry in their heads, and those images come from fairy tales, books, films, songs, museums and memes. Culture stops being a decoration of consumption and becomes the space where people look for direction. This macrotrend is about who now writes the image of the future — and whose image wins.*

## Four worlds

The worlds differ in who writes the image of the future:

| World | Who writes the image of the future | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-cs\|CS]] | Platforms and industries: streaming, agencies, recommendations | Global hits and access to everything | Uniformity; culture as an asset and a dataset |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-ce\|CE]] | The state and institutions: museums, school, big projects | A shared history and memory | A canon: others decide what counts as right |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-ds\|DS]] | The author: a personal hand and a direct link to the audience | Authorship and authenticity | Competing with the machine, and instability |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-de\|DE]] | The community: fandoms, memes, local scenes | A shared language for strangers | Closedness and culture "for our own only" |

A second axis: CS and DS create the new (one for everyone, the other from oneself), while CE and DE keep and remake what is shared (one for the country, the other for one's own people).

## Where we are now

### First you need to change the fairy tales

Our actions rest on an image of the future, and that image is built from the myths and metaphors we receive in childhood. Hence a simple conclusion: to change the future, you need to change the stories that draw it. States and platforms understood this long ago; futures institutes hardly see it.

### What futures institutes changed

- **CIFS**: there is no culture among the 2026 megatrends as a force of its own.
- **Dubai Future Foundation**: "Future Humanity" (2023 → 2026) — technologies are judged through education, access, social ties and wellbeing.
- **VML Future 100**: culture as world-building and shared experience — stories made with AI, new rave scenes. The code of the year is "disoptimism": acknowledging darkness without surrendering to it.
- **Klein, META 2026**: trends are born in small subcultures; spectacle and goosebumps are overrated, authenticity and ordinary life are underrated.

What the institutes don't write: K-pop, anime and "KPop Demon Hunters" are the main carriers of young people's values. States understood this before futurists did.

### Figures

- **The world of one animated film.** "KPop Demon Hunters" spent 52 consecutive weeks in Netflix's global top 10, with about 640 million views. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-039|Signal]]
- **A platform running on AI.** Netflix confirmed that generative AI was used on about 300 titles in 2026. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-024|Signal]]
- **Science fiction as a textbook.** HSE researchers derived future competencies from 500 works of science fiction. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-043|Signal]]
- **A direct link to the author.** 69% of collectors buy directly from artists. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-046|Signal]]
- **Hand-made as a premium.** Prada made its holiday campaign with hand-drawn animation. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-040|Signal]]

### The question the scenarios grow from

If images of the future come from culture, who writes them: a streaming algorithm, a state programme, an individual author or a fan community? And which image will prove stronger? The main tensions up to 2035:

- living authorship or synthetic content;
- a canon or the right to one's own interpretation;
- culture for everyone or for one's own.

The four worlds answer this question differently — see the [table above](#four-worlds).
