*[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery|Human Mastery]] is a macrotrend about how people respond to powerful technology: by proving that their own skill is worth something. This is its version in the **Decentralized — Synthetic** world, where a master creates what doesn't exist yet, because it interests them.*

## 2035. The seventh synthesizer

Leya builds synthesizers out of old phones. Nobody taught her: she pieced the knowledge together from open videos, forums and her own AI assistant, which knows all her notes from the last five years. Her first three synthesizers didn't work, and she understood why only after taking them apart with a stranger, an engineer from a chat group. People rarely buy her instruments, and that doesn't bother her much: she makes them for herself first of all. On Thursdays she runs an online circle for beginners and says honestly where her knowledge ends. Last week her assistant wrote a portfolio description for her, and Leya rewrote half of it because it described things she hadn't done.

## Where we come from

Mastery has always been a ladder: apprentice, journeyman, master. Each wave of automation — machines, the assembly line, computers — took one layer of work from people, and now AI takes the first drafts that newcomers used to learn on. The entry to professions is narrowing: since 2022, noticeably fewer 22–25-year-olds are hired in occupations where AI does much of the work. The DS answer: build the ladder yourself, out of open knowledge and your own attempts. [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In the DS world you decide what to learn, how and why. The master's main question is "what do I really want to make?" Mastery starts with listening to yourself. Whether the world needs it is a secondary question: first of all, you are your own client.

A DS master creates what doesn't exist yet. They assemble their own learning environment: open materials, a personal archive, digital assistants, projects and people they have chosen. Everyone's set of skills is different, and it grows around what the person wants to do.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Freedom and authorship: you do what you love, the way you think is right. You learn fast because you are interested. Your work is yours.

**What it costs.** Nobody guarantees anyone needs it or will pay for it. You need time, money and energy, and nobody hands them out. Without outside pressure it is hard to get through the boring part of learning. Freedom from institutions turns into dependence on platforms and services. And an AI assistant is happy to embellish, so assessing yourself honestly is a separate skill.

## Signals

- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-021|Exercises in Learning]]. Someone built a printing press, learning everything needed on YouTube, and published the documentation back online. One's own task gathers learning around it.
- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-027|College Unbound]]. Adult students build their own study plan around their work and life, and confirm what they learned with a portfolio.
- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-028|School of Machines]]. Short, deep programmes at the crossing of art and technology that end in an exhibition. Technical skill grows together with one's own idea.
- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-026|Bredagora]] and [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-030|Agile Learning Centers]]. Schools where children choose what to do themselves, while mentors and the rhythm of the day help them not give up on the hard parts.
- **Personal AI.** A company offers a model trained on a specialist's own notes and documents. Accumulated experience becomes a personal working tool. [Source](https://www.personal.ai/insights/the-promise-and-pitfalls-of-ai-for-lawyers)

## What the games found

If mastery in DS means listening to yourself and creating your own thing, the games explored how skill actually grows in this world.

### What to develop next

A set of skills doesn't have to grow in every direction. Participants suggested growing in a spiral: strengthen what already works, notice what's missing and add a neighbouring skill. If you keep switching between short courses, it's easy to skip the hardest part every time.

> What else can he strengthen, well, in this particular role? With which skills to strengthen exactly this role, so as not to go too far?

<!-- Q-MT031-011, translated from Russian. Source: second game, line 265. -->

### Do first, figure it out after

A DS master learns by trying: try, meet an explanation or someone else's method, compare the difference. A mistake teaches only if it is followed by a review. Adults were encouraged to return to individual basic skills the same way, such as handwriting, if they themselves want to.

> I also always like to start with a practical action, and then figure out what happened and why.

<!-- Q-MT031-017, translated from Russian. Source: third game, line 1. -->

### Teach others while you learn

You don't need to know a whole field to share what you have already mastered. What matters is naming the limit of your knowledge honestly. Explaining often makes things clear to yourself, and beginners' questions show what you don't know yet.

> So you don't necessarily have to learn everything completely in order to teach.

> I explained it for so long that I understood it myself.

<!-- Q-MT031-018, Q-MT031-019, translated from Russian. Source: third game, line 1. -->

### Effort without coercion

Boring repetition is sometimes needed, but the fact that an activity is unpleasant doesn't prove it's useful. Participants remembered harsh schools that killed the wish to learn, and looked for ways to give themselves discipline without pressure. They found no ready answer. They suggested a manageable rhythm, support and gradually increasing difficulty.

> For all my, well, understanding that it's great to do, I don't think the tools were competent.

> So, like, how to limit it, how to do it, but not go down the path of the Soviet system, by the way.

<!-- Q-MT031-020, Q-MT031-021, translated from Russian. Source: third game, line 1. -->

## What you can do now

1. Name what you really want to make, and which neighbouring skill will bring you closer.
2. Make a manageable attempt, then find an explanation or another method and compare.
3. Share a piece you've mastered with someone who needs it, and name the limit of your knowledge.
4. Check descriptions of your work, especially ones written with AI, against what you actually did.
5. Choose a rhythm that fits your energy and money; drop courses that don't lead to your task.

## Mastery in other worlds

What sets a master apart in each world is who they listen to:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-cs|CS]] — the instruction.** Execute what exists precisely, and don't get bored.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ce|CE]] — the shared goal.** Hear what the big cause needs and find your place in it.
- **DS — yourself.** Create what doesn't exist, because it interests you.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-de|DE]] — the environment.** Keep changing what exists, together with the people around you.
