*[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery|Human Mastery]] is a macrotrend about how people respond to powerful technology: by proving that their own skill is worth something. This is its version in the **Decentralized — Earth** world, where a master keeps changing what exists, together with the people around them.*

## 2035. One building

Timur and a crew of neighbours are turning an old community centre into a neighbourhood workshop. There is no blueprint: they open the walls to see what is left inside, and at the evening meetings the residents want something slightly different each time. He listens, reworks the plan and tries things, and the project becomes clear in the work itself. In the morning he taught a teenager, Artyom, to lay tiles; in the afternoon he asked an elderly neighbour how the building used to be heated. Half the neighbours come every evening, the other half only give advice, and that makes Timur angry. By the end of the month he is exhausted: nothing repeats, and this work can never be finished.

## Where we come from

Mastery has always been a ladder: apprentice, journeyman, master. Each wave of automation — machines, the assembly line, computers — took one layer of work from people, and now AI takes the first drafts that newcomers used to learn on. The entry to professions is narrowing: since 2022, noticeably fewer 22–25-year-olds are hired in occupations where AI does much of the work. The DE answer: learn where automation can't reach — in work with living things, next to other people. [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In the DE world, mastery is work with living things: people, a place, a material. Every time is a little different, which is why it can't be automated. A master doesn't execute what is ready and doesn't create from scratch, but keeps changing what exists.

The main skill here is sensing the environment: reading what the people around need and what meanings a place holds, and starting to create from that. The task can change along the way, and a master has to notice. Mastery lives in the process, in working together and in passing on experience.

This is also where the border with [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-cs|CS]] runs: DE mastery begins where the instruction ends. Someone who notices that automation has stopped working can only report it up the chain in CS; in DE they go and rework it themselves.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Living work that is never boring. You can see your work changing a place and its people. Connection with others: you teach and learn every day.

**What it costs.** The process is endless, and it wears you out. There is no instruction to lean on and no point where you can say "done". Contributions in a community are unequal: some pull, others take. And outside recognition is hard to get in a world where everything is done on the spot.

## What the games found

If mastery in DE means constantly changing what exists while sensing the environment, the games explored how such a master grows and what this work costs.

### The project becomes clear in the making

In DE, an idea is never ready in advance. It appears once you start working with the material and the people, and sometimes the task itself changes along the way. A master has to notice this and not cling to the original plan. But to respond meaningfully to surprises, you still need skills, theory and feedback.

> The project starts to take shape at the moment of making. That's it.

<!-- Q-MT031-004, translated from Russian. Source: first game, line 404. -->

### Breadth and depth

To sense the environment you need a broad outlook: logic, philosophy and culture help you see connections. Such skills are not always immediately practical, but they strengthen everything else. Participants also reminded each other of the reverse: breadth doesn't replace depth where depth is needed. A DE master knows where their training ends and calls in someone who knows more.

> There are skills that give a bonus. To all the others. And most likely they are not obviously practical.

> but there always has to be expertise somewhere too.

<!-- Q-MT031-006, Q-MT031-007, translated from Russian. Source: first game, lines 706, 711. -->

### Quality shows in the consequences

In a world without a checklist, mastery is checked by what changed: does the thing work, could people use it. Participants suggested asking of every stage why it is needed. An intermediate result is valuable if it's clear what it gives the next step.

> And then you need to understand: why am I doing this? Giving every stage its own why.

<!-- Q-MT031-005, translated from Russian. Source: first game, line 692. -->

### Teaching others without burning out

In DE, mastery passes from hand to hand. Participants separated the roles: a mentor helps you learn, the recipient of the result says whether it's needed, an assessor confirms the skill. These don't have to be one person. And an open question remained: how do you keep a community going if some people constantly teach and help while others only take? Participants found no ready system.

> then find a mentor who will walk that path with him, holding his hand, maybe teach him something here and there. Give some advice.

> So where do you find that balance?

<!-- Q-MT031-014, Q-MT031-022, translated from Russian. Sources: second game, line 424; third game, line 1. -->

## What you can do now

1. Start with people: ask what they need and compare it with what interests you.
2. Make a small attempt and look at what changed in the task and in the method.
3. For each stage, name why it exists and what it gives the next step.
4. Separate who is your mentor, who receives the result and who checks the quality.
5. Agree on everyone's contribution in advance, before fatigue builds up.
6. Know the limit of your training and call in someone who knows more.

## Mastery in other worlds

What sets a master apart in each world is who they listen to:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-cs|CS]] — the instruction.** Execute what exists precisely, and don't get bored.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ce|CE]] — the shared goal.** Hear what the big cause needs and find your place in it.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ds|DS]] — yourself.** Create what doesn't exist, because it interests you.
- **DE — the environment.** Keep changing what exists, together with the people around you.
