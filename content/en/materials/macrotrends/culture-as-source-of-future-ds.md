*[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future|Culture as a Source of the Future]] is a macrotrend about who writes the images of the future people live by. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-synthetic|Decentralized — Synthetic]]** world, where an individual author writes them — in their own hand and for their own audience.*

## 2035. A studio in the attic

The artist Vera paints the city a hundred years from now — by hand, in gouache, with blots. Her works aren't sold through a gallery: buyers come to her attic studio or write to her directly, and she knows each of them by name. For each work Vera records a short video — how she mixed the paints and why the sky didn't work the first time. Buyers value these videos most of all: "you can see it's a person". By day Vera has a side job teaching an AI studio what a living brush looks like. In the evening she opens her mail: an architecture firm wants to hang her city in its office — "we need an image of the future, not a render". Vera agrees, but names a price that would once have embarrassed her.

## Where we come from

The author always lived between the patron and the public: a sponsor, a gallery, a publisher, a label. The internet gave a direct link to the audience, and generative AI made "a picture" and "a text" cheap. In response value moved to what a machine cannot repeat: a living hand, the process and the author's story. [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future#where-we-are-now|More — history and figures]].

## What the world becomes

In DS cultural scenes grow from below: independent artists, writers, musicians, world-builders. Technology helps authors build their own routes and formats — from the studio to a subscription. Authenticity is valued: the trace of the hand, the process, the mistake, a personal interpretation. The intermediary leaves or becomes a guarantor of authenticity. Each author has their own image of the future — and that makes it more valuable.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Authorship and authenticity: you decide what future to tell about, and you know the people it matters to.

**What it costs.** Competing with the machine, and instability: synthetic content is cheaper, and the author's income depends on them alone.

## What you can do now

1. Buy at least one thing a year directly from its author — and learn their story.
2. If you create, show the process, not only the result.
3. Draw, write or record your own image of the future — clumsily perhaps, but with your own hands.
4. Look for authors from your own city and region, not only from the global charts.

## Culture as a Source of the Future in other worlds

The worlds differ in who writes the image of the future:
