*[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery|Human Mastery]] is a macrotrend about how people respond to powerful technology: by proving that their own skill is worth something. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|Centralized — Earth]]** world, where a master fits into a big shared cause.*

## 2035. Third year at the academy

Dasha studies at an academy of restorers run by a programme to revive small northern towns. Which houses get saved this year is decided by the programme's council, not by her. Her mentor Ildar has spent thirty years in the trade; he shows everything with his hands and only explains when hands no longer help. For her first two years Dasha tried masonry, calculations and painting and was average at all of them. Then it turned out that nobody at the academy reads old drawings better than she does: now people from other crews come to her. A classmate who talks about the programme brilliantly online already sits on the commission, although he is worse with his hands. Dasha is still about ten years away from the guild's master badge.

## Where we come from

Mastery has always been a ladder: apprentice, journeyman, master. Each wave of automation — machines, the assembly line, computers — took one layer of work from people, and now AI takes the first drafts that newcomers used to learn on. The entry to professions is narrowing: since 2022, noticeably fewer 22–25-year-olds are hired in occupations where AI does much of the work. The CE answer: restore the ladder — school, mentor and recognition by the community — inside a big shared cause. [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In the CE world there is a big goal set by the center: a state, an institution, a guild, a professional school. It defines what is worth knowing and what counts as good work. Mastery here is collective: the work is done together, and a master is the one who heard correctly what the cause needs and found their own unique place in it.

That is the difference from [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-cs|CS]]. There, there is no unique place; here there is, but you have to find it inside a shared frame rather than invent it from scratch. The path is long: school, mentor, practice next to those who are stronger, recognition by the community.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Meaning: you are part of something bigger than yourself. Depth: a long school and mentors give what short courses can't. Recognition: the community knows who you are and what you can do.

**What it costs.** You don't choose the goal, and sometimes your place in the cause is assigned. The path is long and hard to leave. And recognition depends on people: volume and an institution's reputation sometimes outshine the quality of the work.

## Signals

- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-010|Fab Academy]]. One digital fabrication programme for the whole world, but people learn in local workshops, with their own instructors, on their own projects. A shared standard plus a personal contribution.
- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-016|Solar Mamas]]. Women from rural communities, many of them unable to read, master solar engineering and return home as the village's specialists. Mastery is recognised by the work, not by school grades.
- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-019|Design Factory Global Network]]. A university lab where students of different disciplines solve problems for companies and the city. Different skills come together around a shared task.
- **Institute of Professional Accountants of Russia.** The professional association keeps an open register where you can check whether a specialist is certified. The community itself defines and confirms mastery. [Source](https://www.ipbr.org/?id=9&page=partners)

## What the games found

If mastery in CE means finding your place in a shared cause, the games explored how to get there and who decides that you are a master.

### Don't be the smartest person in the room

The first step is to be near those who are stronger. A mentor and working side by side with a master show what you can't see yourself. Participants also noted that experience is not the same as age or status: the skill you need may belong to someone younger than you.

> to grow, you sort of have to, well, not be the smartest one in this room.

<!-- Q-MT031-013, translated from Russian. Source: second game, line 305. -->

### A shared frame and your place in it

A school or guild sets the standard, but what makes you a master is what you contributed yourself. That is how [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-010|Fab Academy]] works: one programme for everyone, a portfolio of your own projects. In the games people said that shared training gives a common language for working with others, and both the cause and the person need it.

### Who decides you are a master

This is CE's most vulnerable spot. Recognition comes from the community, and the community sees more than the work. Participants told how the name of a university outweighs skill, and how a position goes to the most visible person rather than the best one. What they suggested: talk about the way you work — the task, the solution, the mistakes — and check separately what a person can actually do.

> people who weren't very good specialists, but were very good at showing their face — they got hired

> But I think the university's reputation should be pushed aside and an independent assessment carried out as well.

<!-- Q-MT031-012, Q-MT031-002, translated from Russian. Sources: second game, line 302; first game, line 487. -->

### Hands-on work is mastery too

Participants argued against the idea that working with your hands is a simple path with nothing more to learn. Materials, tools and tasks change, and a practical profession needs constant updating. [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-016|Solar Mamas]] show that such mastery can be learned without the usual school filter, if a specific community needs it.

## What you can do now

1. Find an environment where you are not the strongest, and name exactly what you want to learn there.
2. Work out what your chosen field requires: what to learn, how to show your skill and what licence is needed.
3. Try several roles in the shared cause and notice where your contribution is irreplaceable.
4. Show not only the result but the way you work: the task, the solution, the mistakes.
5. When choosing a specialist, look at their work, not only at their diploma and fame.

## Mastery in other worlds

What sets a master apart in each world is who they listen to:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-cs|CS]] — the instruction.** Execute what exists precisely, and don't get bored.
- **CE — the shared goal.** Hear what the big cause needs and find your place in it.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ds|DS]] — yourself.** Create what doesn't exist, because it interests you.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-de|DE]] — the environment.** Keep changing what exists, together with the people around you.
