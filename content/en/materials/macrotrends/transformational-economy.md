*When life gets more expensive, inequality grows and growth stops being the norm, people get dizzy from one question: what is actually worth money? Value moves from things to meaning, trust, time and the right to control. This macrotrend is about who now decides what things are worth.*

## Four worlds

The worlds differ in who decides what things are worth:

| World | Who decides what things are worth | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-cs\|CS]] | An algorithm and a platform: price, rating, asset value | A clear ladder and convenience | You are rated rather than rating |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-ce\|CE]] | The system: what counts as enough and what to protect | Security and clear norms | Narrower choice; extras are not for everyone |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-ds\|DS]] | You: your signature, your name, intangible capital | Freedom and a direct link to the buyer | Instability; everything rests on you |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-de\|DE]] | Your own people: the close circle, exchange, gifts | Trust and a life of “enough” | A narrow circle and closedness |

A second axis: CS and DS count value in money (one through a platform, the other through a personal name), while CE and DE count it in resilience (one for the country, the other for one’s own people).

## Where we are now

### Value Vertigo

The name comes from Matt Klein’s META Trends 2025 review. The rising cost of living, inequality and limited mobility made “what is really worth money” the central question. This drives two movements at once: pragmatic saving and a willingness to pay for meaning, status, quality and a sense of control. Luxury is no longer a thing but time, attention, restraint and access to your own people.

### What futures institutes changed

- **CIFS** (Copenhagen Institute for Futures Studies): “Economic Growth” and “Service Economy” (2022) became **“The Constrained Economy”** (2026). Growth is no longer treated as the norm; the main question is what constrains it: resources, capital, labour, infrastructure, geopolitics. “Concentration of Wealth” stayed and became sharper.
- **Dubai Future Foundation**: Global 50 2026 added a separate megatrend on money, **“Redefining Finance and Monetary Systems”**: tokenisation, central-bank digital currencies, stablecoins. “Energy Boundaries” moved from megatrends to assumptions — a few months before the Strait of Hormuz closed.
- **IFTF** (Institute for the Future, Palo Alto) writes about an economy redesigned around mutualism instead of financialisation, and about the “gamblification of everyday life”.
- **VML Future 100**: from the escapism of 2025 to the “transformative experiences” of 2026 — 86% are drawn to experiences that evoke awe.

In the lecture of 11 November 2026 we put it this way: a constrained economy means growth only with regard to local possibilities, the global situation and people’s expectations.

### Figures

- **Resources became the bottleneck.** About a third of seaborne fertiliser trade passed through the Strait of Hormuz; after it closed on 28 February 2026, urea roughly doubled in price by April. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-021|Signal]]
- **Countries hold on to raw materials.** Since August 2026 the DR Congo has banned exports of copper and cobalt concentrate. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-020|Signal]]
- **Culture becomes collateral.** About 20% of wealthy collectors have borrowed against art, 45% in the US. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-047|Signal]]
- **Freelance income splits.** Fiverr lost 21.9% of its buyers in a year, while 5.6 million US independents earn over $100,000. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-008|Signal]] · [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-006|Signal]]
- **Expectations are an economic force.** The 2026 Nobel Prize in economics: household decisions depend on the future people expect. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-044|Signal]]

### The question the scenarios grow from

If growth is no longer promised and money, resources and attention are limited, what counts as value, who confirms it and what does a person pay for their footing? The main tensions up to 2035:

- saving, or paying for meaning;
- value counted for you, or value you create yourself;
- trust in the system, or trust in your own people.

The four worlds answer this question differently — see [the table above](#four-worlds).

## More

**Anchor trends by world (from the base):** Mighty Middle — resilient businesses (CS); discipline through community (CE); the gift as a post-capitalist lever of value (DS); the shift to local communities (DE).

**Adjacent macrotrends:** new drivers of growth; wellbeing economy and alternative growth metrics (Beyond GDP); social business and the common-good economy; post-growth; [[en/materials/trends/tr-2026-11-20|constrained economy and fragmentation]].

**Life domains:** money and material stability, everyday spending, status and identity, a sense of security and control, trust in brands and institutions.

**Industries:** retail and FMCG, finance and consumer credit, real estate and renting, subscription services, media and the creator economy, brand strategy.
