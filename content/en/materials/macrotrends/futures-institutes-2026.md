*In 2026 the big futures institutes rewrote their lists. The main shift is from hope to constraint and control: growth, globalisation and freedom are no longer described as the norm. What matters is not only what they added but what they dropped. Here are the "before → after" pairs and the Future Decoder macrotrends they affect.*

## Before → after

|---|---|---|---|---|

## What they dropped

A topic disappearing is a signal too. Hopes are being removed from the lists:

- **growth** (CIFS) → constraints;
- **globalisation** (CIFS) → a world of power centres;
- **freedom** in individualism (CIFS) → loneliness and fragmentation;
- **the network as cooperation** (CIFS) → the network as power;
- **Web3 and decentralisation** (McKinsey);
- **democratised AI** (Gartner) → control over agents;
- **energy** as a force of its own (Dubai Future Foundation) → an assumption that didn't last six months.

## How this moves the four worlds

Institutes write mainly for centres — states and corporations — so they describe the CS and CE worlds best:

- **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-synthetic|CS]]** — control moves into things; agents, compute and cultural platforms have specific owners.
- **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|CE]]** — states talk about values and culture and invest in the physical world: energy, land, people.
- **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-synthetic|DS]]** — autonomy is deflating: one person can't carry the resources, and freedom becomes freedom of one's own thinking.
- **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|DE]]** — self-organisation without a sponsor: Discord parliaments, fandoms, local scenes. This world is almost absent from the reports.

## What the institutes don't see

- **Culture** as the main source of images of the future: K-pop, anime, Netflix, fairy tales and science fiction.
- **Young people and micro-communities** — described as an audience, not as a force.
- **Resistance**: protests against data centres and the demand for a "right to switch off".
- **The ordinary person**: the 2026 Nobel in economics is about household expectations deciding whether the centre's policy works.
