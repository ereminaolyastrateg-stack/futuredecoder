*[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future|Culture as a Source of the Future]] is a macrotrend about who writes the images of the future people live by. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|Centralized — Earth]]** world, where they are written by the state and cultural institutions.*

## 2035. A new fairy tale

Teacher Elena teaches a third-year class under a new reading programme. It includes the old fairy tales and ten new ones, written on the ministry's commission by children's authors together with scholars. In the new tales three brothers don't compete over who is cleverest; they build a bridge over the river together. After lessons the class goes to the local history museum: an exhibition has opened about the people who lived on this street a hundred years ago. The children recognise their own houses in the old photographs. Elena likes that the class has a shared history. But one boy, Misha, asks why his favourite anime hero isn't in the new tales. Elena answers that he isn't in the programme. Misha draws him in the margins.

## Where we come from

States have always understood the power of stories: school textbooks, museums, monuments, state cinema. In the era of global platforms this power moved to streaming and fandoms. Now states are coming back: investing in cultural industries, writing value strategies and holding big forums about the future. [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future#where-we-are-now|More — history and figures]].

## What the world becomes

In CE culture is part of public infrastructure. Museums, theatres, libraries and humanities education become institutions of shared cohesion and value navigation. The state invests in animation, film, games and science fiction so that the image of the future matches its values. Culture shapes taste, memory and responsibility over a long cycle — and offers a shared path.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** A shared history and memory: people have something to lean on, and the country has a shared language and a shared image of the future.

**What it costs.** A canon. Others decide what counts as the right hero and the right story, and everything outside the programme stays in the margins.

## What you can do now

1. Recall three stories from childhood that still shape what you expect from the future.
2. Take your children to a local museum and find the story of your street or family in it.
3. Suggest a book to your school or library that shows a future you'd want to live in.
4. Read your country's and region's strategies and programmes on culture: what image of the future is built into them.

## Culture as a Source of the Future in other worlds

The worlds differ in who writes the image of the future:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-cs|CS]] — platforms.** Streaming, agencies and recommendations.
- **CE — the state.** Museums, school, big cultural projects and values.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-ds|DS]] — the author.** A personal hand and a direct link to the audience.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-de|DE]] — the community.** Fandoms, memes and local scenes.
