*Data and digital literacy, open knowledge, skill set transition and digital reputation are becoming the basis of intellectual capital. This macrotrend is about who leads your learning path and who recognises the result when knowledge is open to everyone and AI is ready to explain anything.*

## Four worlds

What sets education apart in each world is who leads the learning path and who recognises the result:

## Where we are now

### Five waves of access to knowledge

1. **Monastery and university (12th–15th centuries).** Knowledge is rare and belongs to institutions. To learn means to enter a school and receive its recognition.
2. **The printing press (from the 15th century).** Books become available, and you can learn on your own. But recognition is still granted by universities and guilds.
3. **Mass schooling (19th century).** Compulsory education, age-based classes, a single curriculum and grades. Education becomes a standard for everyone.
4. **The internet and online courses (2000s–2010s).** Knowledge is almost free and available at any moment. The diploma remains the main pass.
5. **The AI tutor (since 2022).** Not only knowledge but explanation is now available: a machine will go through a problem as many times as needed — and write the assignment for you.

Each wave made knowledge more accessible, while recognition stayed with institutions. Today access is no longer scarce. What is scarce is **understanding** — how to check that a person has understood for themselves — and **recognition**: who decides that you can do something, and on what basis.

### The numbers

- **AI in learning has become the norm.** In a December 2025 survey by the UK Higher Education Policy Institute (HEPI), 95% of students said they use AI, and 94% that they use generative AI in assessed work. Only 36% feel encouraged by their university to do so. [Source](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)
- **The degree was devalued in words, not in practice.** A Burning Glass Institute and Harvard Business School study found that companies that dropped degree requirements from job postings barely changed whom they actually hired, and 45% of firms moved to skills-based hiring "in name only". [Source](https://gof.mit.edu/when-firms-drop-college-degree-requirements-does-hiring-change-a-new-study-gives-stark-answers)

### The question the scenarios grow from

If knowledge and explanation are available to everyone, who now decides what and how to learn, how do we know a person really understood, and who recognises the result? The four worlds answer differently — that is the [table above](#four-worlds).

## More detail

**What the macrotrend brings together.** A shift from the diploma as status to skills that can be proven in action. The growth of open knowledge and new search interfaces. Constant reskilling under the pressure of AI. The return of mentoring and learning through participation. Digital reputation, skills passports and alternative forms of credentialing.

**Five different functions.** Learning, practising a skill, confirming an ability, professional licensing and public recognition can happen in different places. It helps to distinguish who helps you learn, who checks the result, who sets the requirements and who decides on access.

**Related megatrends:** new work; AI and automation; slowing economic growth.

**Industries:** education and edtech, corporate learning, HR and talent development, knowledge platforms, schools of mastery, local workshops, mentoring networks.

**Keywords:** data literacy, digital literacy, open knowledge, skill set transition, upskill, reskill, lifelong learning, digital reputation, skills passports, credentialing, meta-skills, mentoring, peer learning, learning-to-learn.
