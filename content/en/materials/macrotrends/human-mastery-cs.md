*[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery|Human Mastery]] is a macrotrend about how people respond to powerful technology: by proving that their own skill is worth something. This is its version in the **Centralized — Synthetic** world, where a master precisely executes what already exists.*

## 2035. One shift

In the morning Nina receives forty contracts the bank's algorithm prepared overnight, and a twelve-point checklist that someone else wrote. She doesn't need to invent anything, and there is a pleasure in that: put on music, find the rhythm and catch the mistakes the machine makes less and less often. Around noon she notices she signed the last five contracts almost without reading them, and goes back. In the evening senior controller Vadim spot-checks her reviews, and his report is read by someone Nina has never seen. One floor up, analyst Sveta has spent two months looking at customer complaints: the contracts are flawless by the checklist, but they don't suit people. She writes this up the chain. Three weeks later everyone receives a new checklist.

## Where we come from

Mastery has always been a ladder: apprentice, journeyman, master. Each wave of automation — machines, the assembly line, computers — took one layer of work from people, and now AI takes the first drafts that newcomers used to learn on. The entry to professions is narrowing: since 2022, noticeably fewer 22–25-year-olds are hired in occupations where AI does much of the work. The CS answer: mastery is precise execution, and instead of a ladder there is a chain of checks. [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In the CS world nothing belongs to you: not the task, not the method, not the result. The system sets the goal, the algorithm does the rough work — the first draft of a contract, code, a report. Junior tasks have gone to the machine. What is expected from a person is precision.

Mastery here means understanding the instruction without error and carrying it out without loss: catching the machine's mistake and not adding anything extra. A master has no unique place of their own. Mastery is exactly about standing well in a place where you create nothing of your own.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** You don't have to invent all the time. You can surrender to what you are told and enjoy precise work, like a surgeon or a pilot working through a checklist. People don't always want to create, and in worlds built on authorship that gets tiring.

**What it costs.** There is almost no creativity, and boredom comes easily. Boredom is dangerous here: the person at the end of the chain stops reading and simply signs. So the second skill of a CS master is staying attentive where nothing changes.

- [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-009|An Automatic Workshop]] (2018). A workshop is run without a facilitator, through instructions and materials. The organiser's knowledge can be packed into instructions and passed on.

## What the games found

If mastery in CS means executing a command precisely and checking how others executed it, the games followed this logic to the end. The result is a ladder of four steps, each with its own person on it.

### How do you know you understood the command correctly

An executor's mastery rests on understanding the instruction precisely. But a smooth answer, especially one produced by a machine, can formally fit the task and say nothing about whether the person understood anything. Participants suggested a simple check: explain the task in your own words and show how the answer relates to it. If you can't explain it, you executed it but didn't understand it.

> How did you understand that you understood?

<!-- Q-MT031-016, translated from Russian. Source: third game, line 1, chars 27482–27509. -->

### You became the checker — who checks you

The next step is checking the work of others, people or algorithms. But in this world the checker is just as much an executor: someone else gave them the criteria too. So the question arises: who assessed them? Participants suggested looking not at titles but at how a person works: a test task and an explanation of the approach. In one example, the task was deliberately made unusable commercially, so that it tested thinking rather than handing the company free work.

> those agencies have to get their experts from somewhere too. How will they assess those experts?

> they are really aimed at assessing how I work and how I think.

<!-- Q-MT031-008, Q-MT031-010, translated from Russian. Source: second game, lines 225, 234. -->

### Everyone checks everyone

Taken to its end, this is a world of constant checking: the machine does the work, a person checks the machine, another person checks the checker. Nobody has authorship, everybody has a signature. The chain works as long as every link actually reads. The weak spot is clear: the link that got bored and signs without looking.

### The one who sees where automation stops working

The rarest and most valuable role in CS. A checker catches an error within the rules, while this person sees that the rules themselves no longer match life: everything is done by the checklist, yet the result is bad. In CS, changing the rules is the center's right, so this person doesn't change them but signals up the chain, and the system issues a new instruction. This is how CS evolves: it needs people with a [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-de|DE]] way of seeing at its edges, where automation runs into living things.

## What you can do now

1. Before handing a task to an algorithm, name what exactly a person will check in the result.
2. Check understanding by asking for an explanation in one's own words, not by how smooth the text is.
3. Ask how the checker works and what tools they use.
4. In a chain of checks, name the link where a person makes the decision, and make sure they actually read.
5. Create a channel for saying "this instruction no longer works", and listen to those who use it.

## Mastery in other worlds

What sets a master apart in each world is who they listen to:

- **CS — the instruction.** Execute what exists precisely, and don't get bored.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ce|CE]] — the shared goal.** Hear what the big cause needs and find your place in it.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ds|DS]] — yourself.** Create what doesn't exist, because it interests you.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-de|DE]] — the environment.** Keep changing what exists, together with the people around you.
