*[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities|Digital Communities]] is a macrotrend about how the network turned from a way of talking into a form of power. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|Decentralized — Earth]]** world, where the members themselves hold the rules and everyone becomes the sponsor.*

## 2035. Voting till morning

Mira's town has had no proper heating for three weeks. Nobody called a meeting — someone in her favourite band's fan chat just asked, "Is it cold where you are too?" A day later the chat has five thousand people from different districts, and each district has its own role, channel and moderator. They vote on whom to send to talk to the local authority, and they vote till morning. They come to the meeting with lightsticks — they have no other symbols. Mira is proud: nobody was giving them orders. Her older brother Gleb argues: the neighbouring chat decided differently, and now the two communities don't talk to each other. "We're ours, they aren't," someone writes in the chat. Mira isn't sure that's right.

## Where we come from

People have always united from below: communes, cooperatives, circles, hobby clubs. Digital communities and fandoms gave this ancient skill a new speed: roles, votes, shared symbols, coordinating thousands of people within hours. Futures institutes still describe them as communication and marketing. [[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities#where-we-are-now|More — history and figures]].

## What the world becomes

In DE a digital community is civic infrastructure. Members assign roles, vote, help each other and take to the street. A shared world — a fandom, a game, a meme — replaces a shared ideology and gives a language to people who don't know each other. The new elite here is not politicians but idols, world-builders and leaders of local communities. The strength of such communities is the absence of a sponsor; the weakness is that they easily split into "ours" and "theirs".

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Power without a sponsor. A community can quickly gather, agree and get what it wants without waiting for the system.

**What it costs.** Chaos and polarisation: belonging matters more than being right, and communities close off from one another. The risk is closed clubs for insiders that you can't get into.

## What you can do now

1. Find one community nearby — your block, a school, a fandom, a club — and become a member, not an observer.
2. Agree in your community on rules: how decisions are made and how new people join.
3. Build a bridge to a neighbouring community you disagree with: a shared project, a shared channel.
4. Learn from fandoms: roles, votes, shared symbols — these are self-organisation skills.

## Digital Communities in other worlds

The worlds differ in who holds the rules of a community:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-cs|CS]] — the platform.** The algorithm and the owner decide whom you see.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-ce|CE]] — the state.** Communities live in a shared frame and national infrastructure.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-ds|DS]] — the creator.** Your own server, your own subscription, your own rules.
- **DE — the members.** A community without a sponsor, where everyone is the sponsor.
