*[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future|Culture as a Source of the Future]] is a macrotrend about who writes the images of the future people live by. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|Decentralized — Earth]]** world, where the community itself writes them — in fandoms, memes and local scenes.*

## 2035. A song in the square

On Saturday a local band plays in the square of a small town: a jaw harp, a guitar, electronics and lyrics in the grandmothers' language. Everyone knows the song — it spread through the district chats before it was released. Teenagers have come with home-made flags from their favourite anime: that's how they recognise their own. Next to them is a stand of children's drawings, "Our town in a hundred years" — collected by the school fan club, nobody commissioned them. Aigul, a music teacher, films it all on her phone and thinks there is more future here than in any report. A colleague from the next town writes: "Why weren't we invited?" Aigul realises that only the people in the chat knew about the concert.

## Where we come from

Culture was always born in communities: songs, rituals, fairy tales, crafts. Industry made it a commodity, and platforms made it global. Now communities are returning in digital form: fandoms, memes, local scenes and shared worlds give people a language in which they recognise their own. [[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future#where-we-are-now|More — history and figures]].

## What the world becomes

In DE culture is a practice of closeness: workshops, small museums, local scenes, fan clubs, memes that everyone draws. A shared world — an anime, a game, a song — becomes a symbol and a language of dissent. Creators are the first to offer new images of the future, and the community picks them up without a sponsor. Regions and small towns become a source of trends. The risk is culture for one's own only: whoever isn't in the chat doesn't know.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** A shared language for people who don't know each other. Culture as an everyday practice of meaning and belonging.

**What it costs.** Closedness: culture "for our own" doesn't reach outsiders, and scenes easily split.

## What you can do now

1. Find a local cultural scene — a band, a workshop, a fan club — and go at least once.
2. Collect drawings "our town in a hundred years" with children or friends, and show them not only to your own.
3. Ask teenagers which worlds and heroes they share — and watch at least one.
4. If you run an event for your own people, leave an open door for those who aren't in the chat.

## Culture as a Source of the Future in other worlds

The worlds differ in who writes the image of the future:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-cs|CS]] — platforms.** Streaming, agencies and recommendations.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-ce|CE]] — the state.** Museums, school, big cultural projects and values.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/culture-as-source-of-future-ds|DS]] — the author.** A personal hand and a direct link to the audience.
- **DE — the community.** Fandoms, memes and local scenes.
