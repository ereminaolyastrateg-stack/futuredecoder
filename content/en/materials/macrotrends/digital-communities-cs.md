*[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities|Digital Communities]] is a macrotrend about how the network turned from a way of talking into a form of power. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-synthetic|Centralized — Synthetic]]** world, where the platform and its owner hold the rules of a community.*

## 2035. A fandom by subscription

Fourteen-year-old Liza is a member of her band's official fan club. The club lives in the agency's app: concerts, votes, private videos and a ranking of the most active fans. To get into the top hundred, Liza watches a clip, votes and buys digital cards every day. Her AI companion, also from the app, praises her "streak" and reminds her there are two votes left before the end of the week. In the evening the app suggests a new band from the same agency — "you'll like it". Liza doesn't notice that a friend from another fandom disappeared from her feed long ago. Her mum, Olga, once looks at the payment history and can't work out what it was all for.

## Where we come from

Social networks began as a place where people found each other. Then an algorithm took over the feed, and communities moved onto platforms that earn from their attention. A fandom, a stream, a server — each has an owner. [[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities#where-we-are-now|More — history and figures]].

## What the world becomes

In CS a community is a platform product. The algorithm decides whom you see, whom you befriend and what you are offered. Talking turns into a flow of content, purchases and micropayments, and emotional support comes from digital companions. Communities can be gathered, grown and monetised — and they are owned by companies, funds and agencies.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Huge reach and ready-made tools: you find your people in a minute, everything is convenient, good-looking and works without effort.

**What it costs.** The owner changes the rules. The community's data, ties and money belong to the platform, and the owner's interests may not match the members'.

In CS communities live inside agents and recommendations. So power belongs to whoever runs them.

## What you can do now

1. Check which platforms your communities live on and who owns those platforms.
2. Keep a backup channel with important people — not only inside one app.
3. Check what the recommendations have hidden: which friends you haven't seen in your feed for a long time.
4. Go through with your children what a fan ranking is and what the agency earns on.

## Digital Communities in other worlds

The worlds differ in who holds the rules of a community:

- **CS — the platform.** The algorithm and the owner decide whom you see.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-ce|CE]] — the state.** Communities live in a shared frame and national infrastructure.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-ds|DS]] — the creator.** Your own server, your own subscription, your own rules.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-de|DE]] — the members.** A community without a sponsor, where everyone is the sponsor.
