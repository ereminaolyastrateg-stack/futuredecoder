*[[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy|Education, Knowledge and Literacy]] is a macrotrend about who leads the learning path and who recognises the result when knowledge is open to all. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|Decentralized — Earth]]** world, where knowledge is born in a shared cause.*

## 2035. The river behind the school

For three months Vera and her classmates have been studying the river near their neighbourhood. They work with biologists from the university and with old fishermen who remember what the river used to be like. There is no subject timetable: maths and chemistry come in when they can't do without them. When it became clear the old sampling plan gave nothing, the class rewrote it themselves at a general meeting. The data went to the city council, and for the first time the river was discussed seriously there. Vera's father asks how this will count for university admission — nobody knows for sure yet.

## Where we come from

Each wave — the university, the book, mass schooling, the internet — made knowledge more accessible while leaving recognition to institutions. Now AI explains anything, and what is scarce is no longer knowledge but understanding and recognition. The DE answer: learn where knowledge isn't ready yet — in a shared cause, with living people and real consequences; recognition comes from the environment the work was done for. [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

Learning arises from working with a task, material, people and the consequences of decisions. Participants try, get a response, refine the method and sometimes change the question itself. Knowledge passes through participation and joint research; theory and mentoring come in when they're needed. The original image of this world is the **"author of one's own life"**: knowledge is passed on through living practice in a community.

Signals widen this image: a learner can take part in creating new knowledge and the very way of learning, and a community can discuss and revise the rules. That takes real powers, resources and agreements about the consequences of trying.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Learning is needed not "later" but now: the people around see its result. Knowledge is alive and tied to a place. You learn together with others and pass on what you've learned straight away.

**What it costs.** Beginners need support structures, and they are easy to lose in constant movement. Participation takes a lot of energy. It's unclear who may change the rules. And familiar recognition — diplomas, points — fits this kind of experience poorly so far.

## What the games found

If in DE knowledge is born in a shared cause, the games explored how it is checked and who decides within it.

### The learning task can change along the way

Attention shifted from passing on content to interaction: what learners do, whom they meet, what response they get. Material, a mistake, another person or an unexpected result can change the original question. It matters to notice that the old task no longer fits. But spontaneity doesn't replace mastering something: it takes time, explanation and support for beginners.

### Taking part and changing the rules are different rights

Around [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-039|Rightful Presence and Radical Making]] people discussed learners as co-authors of their learning environment: influence on criteria, resources and the rules of shared work. But taking part in a workshop doesn't give the right to a final decision. You need to know who accepts changes, whom an experiment affects and how a failed agreement is revised. Without that, a joint experiment easily turns into carrying out someone else's assignment.

### The task for a skill is found together with people

Around [[en/materials/signals/education-signals/sig-edu-011|The Pluriversity]] learning was linked to the needs of a region. Participants suggested looking around, talking to people and matching their needs with your own interests and skills. This helps find an application but doesn't guarantee demand for any skill. If the nearest agenda doesn't resonate, the circle of search can be widened.

### A changing practice needs support

There was an objection to constantly abandoning every form. To learn and get a result, you need to hold an agreement, a way of working or a direction long enough. A support isn't a ban on revising it. Participants suggested combining lasting values with small trials, and keeping the right to reconsider the load if there are no resources to continue.

## What you can do now

1. Find a task of specific people or a place and match it with your interest.
2. Talk to those who need the result and clarify what really matters to them.
3. Make a manageable trial and look at what changed in the task and the way of working.
4. Agree who may change the rules of the shared work and how a failed decision is revised.
5. Record the result and its limits, pass on what you've learned and collect others' questions.
