*People live less and less in shared feeds and more and more in small communities: servers, chats, fandoms, clubs. These communities have learned to negotiate, vote and take to the streets. This macrotrend is about how the network turned from a way of talking into a form of power — and about who holds its rules.*

## Four worlds

The worlds differ in who holds the rules of a community:

| World | Who holds the rules | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-cs\|CS]] | The platform and its owner | Huge reach and ready-made tools | The owner changes the rules; the data is theirs |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-ce\|CE]] | The state and institutions | Security and a shared frame | The frame is set in advance; leaving is costly |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-ds\|DS]] | The creator: author, host, server owner | Control over attention and income | Everything rests on one person |
| [[en/materials/macrotrends/digital-communities-de\|DE]] | The members themselves | Power without a sponsor | Chaos, polarisation and closed clubs |

A second axis: in CS and CE the rules come from above (from a company or the state), in DS and DE from inside (from one creator or from everyone together).

## Where we are now

### From the feed to the server

For twenty years social networks were built around a shared feed and reach. Then the metaverse peak passed, and people moved into closed formats: Discord servers, chats, subscriptions, clubs, fandoms. Value is created through belonging and direct ties rather than reach. At the same time recommendation algorithms deepen the fragmentation of attention and trust.

### What futures institutes changed

- **CIFS**: "Network Economy" (2022) became **"Power Through Networks"** (2026). The network used to be described as a way to cooperate and create value; now it is an infrastructure of power in its own right, reaching beyond states. "Individualisation & Empowerment" became "Individualised Everyday Life": smaller households, living alone, fragmentation.
- **FTSG**: instead of trends about the metaverse and "one-to-few networks" (2023), the convergence **"Emotional Outsourcing"** (2026): comfort and companionship move to digital systems.
- **IFTF**: "the loneliness business is booming" and "the human verification crisis".
- **VML Future 100**: social health and new rave scenes — community and shared experience are worth more than ownership.

What the institutes don't write: for them digital communities are communication and marketing. Outside the reports, communities have already become political and civic infrastructure.

### Figures

- **A Discord server as a parliament.** In Nepal in September 2025 more than 10,000 server members voted for an interim prime minister. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-037|Signal]]
- **Fandom skills enter civic life.** In Korea the lightsticks and chants of K-pop fans came to the 2024–2025 protests. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-038|Signal]]
- **A shared symbol above ideologies.** The One Piece flag appeared at youth protests from Nepal to France. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-035|Signal]]
- **Communities have owners.** Tencent is the second-largest shareholder of SM Entertainment; Kick was created by the founders of the crypto casino Stake. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-036|Signal]]
- **Intimacy with machines.** 49% of Gen Z speak of "meaningful relationships" with AI. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-013|Signal]]

### The question the scenarios grow from

If a community can agree, vote and take to the street, whose power is it: the platform it lives on, the state whose frame it acts within, the author it gathered around, or the members themselves? The main tensions up to 2035:

- belonging or being right: "we are ours" against "who is right";
- the community's autonomy or the platform's rules;
- openness or a closed club.

The four worlds answer this question differently — see the [table above](#four-worlds).
