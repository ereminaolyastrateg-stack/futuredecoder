*[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy|Transformational Economy]] is a macrotrend about how the idea of value changes when growth is no longer the norm. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|Centralized — Earth]]** world, where the system decides what counts as enough and what must be protected.*

## 2035. A season on quotas

Farmer Pyotr gets fertiliser on a quota: this spring there is less again, but the price is fixed for three years. An agronomist from the district centre helps him work out how much each hectare needs so that nothing is wasted. In the city his daughter Anya pays for her flat at a discounted rate for young families, on condition that they stay in the region for another five years. Anya is happy: they have a home and a kindergarten nearby. But for two years she hasn't bought a new phone — "extras" are taxed, and the local plant makes its own, simpler model. In the evening the family chat discusses the news: the country no longer exports metal and grain without processing them first. Pyotr says: "So there will be work." Anya thinks that studying abroad has become harder.

## Where we come from

The promise of the twentieth century went like this: the economy grows, so tomorrow everyone will have more. In the 2020s this promise cracked: expensive energy, shortages of resources, competition between countries. Futures institutes recorded it: CIFS turned "economic growth" into "the constrained economy". [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In CE, value is whatever helps the system hold out: land, water, energy, food, people. The state decides what to protect, what not to let out of the country and what counts as a sufficient life. Consumption becomes a discipline: usefulness, quality, durability and trusted communities matter more than status. Growth is possible, but only within local capacities and a shared goal.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Security. Prices of essentials are predictable, work is nearby, a family has a long horizon. It is clear what is valued, and there is no need to chase fashion.

**What it costs.** Choice narrows. Extras are not for everyone, the norms are not set by you, and leaving or stepping outside the rules is expensive.

## Signals

- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-048|CIFS rewrote its megatrends]]. Constraints and a shifting world order instead of growth and globalisation.
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-049|Dubai Future Foundation dropped energy from its megatrends]]. Energy was treated as a settled question — and a few months later it became the year's main risk.
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-020|DR Congo banned concentrate exports]]. Sovereignty over resources is asserted by ban.
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-021|Hormuz cut off fertiliser]]. An energy crisis becomes a food crisis within months.
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-044|The 2026 Nobel for expectations]]. The centre's promises work only if people believe them.

## What was said at the lecture

### Growth only within constraints

CIFS no longer writes about economic growth as the norm. At the lecture we read it this way: growth is possible only with regard to local capacities, the situation in the world and people's expectations.

> They write that this is a kind of constrained economy.

<!-- Lecture «The Future Has Changed Hands», 11.11.2026, on the CIFS megatrends. Translated from Russian. -->

### Expectation is a resource too

The centre depends heavily on people: its promises work as long as they are believed. So in CE what is valued is not only land and energy but also trust in the system.

> The centre depends heavily on us, because the central bank's promises only work if people believe them

<!-- Same source, on the 2026 Nobel Prize. -->

### Not "give us more" but "change the rules"

When housing is out of reach for most people, they start demanding not money from the system but different rules. This is the request CE has to answer to stay legitimate.

> we are used to solving our housing problems on our own, but if most people can't afford housing, it seems to me that's a problem of the system

<!-- Same source, the speaker's retelling of the agenda of the protests in Spain. -->

## What you can do now

1. Work out which resources your life and work depend on — energy, food, housing, medicines — and where they are produced.
2. Find out which long-term programmes exist in your region — housing, education, family support — and what they ask in return.
3. Buy less often but for longer: quality and repairability.
4. Take part in local discussions of the rules, where they can still be changed.

## Transformational Economy in other worlds

The worlds differ in who decides what things are worth:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-cs|CS]] — the algorithm.** Price, rating and asset value.
- **CE — the system.** What counts as enough and what to protect.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-ds|DS]] — you yourself.** Your hand, your name and a direct link to the buyer.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-de|DE]] — your own people.** Exchange, gifts and a life that is "enough".
