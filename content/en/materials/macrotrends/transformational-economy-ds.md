*[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy|Transformational Economy]] is a macrotrend about how the idea of value changes when growth is no longer the norm. This is its version in the **[[en/four-worlds/decentralized-synthetic|Decentralized — Synthetic]]** world, where a person creates value themselves — with their own hand, their name and a direct link to those who buy.*

## 2035. Forty subscribers

For five years Lyosha wrote texts to order through a freelance marketplace. Then orders for simple texts dried up: AI does them now. Today he has forty subscribers who pay every month for his pieces on city stories — with his intonation, his walks and his mistakes. He earns less than before, but the money is his. In the morning he records a voice note for subscribers, in the afternoon he helps a friend's studio teach its AI agent to "write like a human", in the evening he gets a tip from a reader in another country. His former classmate Dima went to an agency: three projects on a salary, guarantees, but no name of his own. They argue about which of them is freer. Lyosha thinks he is. Dima reminds him that Lyosha has no sick pay.

## Where we come from

The DS world began with a promise: anyone can work for themselves and choose their orders, place and time. Freelance marketplaces and creator platforms opened this door to millions. Now this world is splitting: AI takes the simple tasks, and those with a name of their own win. [[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy#where-we-are-now|More — history and numbers]].

## What the world becomes

In DS, value is what only you can do: your hand, taste, view and relationship with your audience. Intangible capital — reputation, network, symbolic status — competes with money. Gifts, tips and subscriptions become ways of exchange. The intermediary between author and buyer disappears or changes its role: it is now responsible not for matching and delivery but for trust and quality.

## What it gives and what it costs

**What it gives.** Freedom and a direct link: you decide what to do and for how much, and you know who pays.

**What it costs.** Instability: income depends on you alone, with no guarantees. The split grows: at the top are those with a name, at the bottom those whose work a machine easily replaces.

## Signals

- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-008|Global freelancing is shrinking]]. Fiverr has 21.9% fewer buyers; in 43 of 63 categories, tasks people already do in ChatGPT are losing their share of orders.
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-006|Independent workers split]]. 5.6 million US independents earn over $100,000, but 30% of newcomers arrived "with almost no choice".
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-046|69% of collectors buy directly from artists]]. The showroom middleman fades; a direct link to the maker remains.
- [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-045|Agents do not remove intermediaries]]. The layer changes function: from passing things on to guaranteeing them.

## What was said at the lecture

### Autonomy is deflating

At the lecture the DS world was called a litmus test of change: when it has many free people, change is close. Now it is deflating — a single person cannot carry physical resources alone.

> We have entered a cycle of change, so the scenario seems to have played its role, and it is starting to deflate.

### Those with their own hand remain

If a machine can repeat the work, it gets cheaper. What stays valuable is what is tied to the person: creativity, the ability to invent.

> And those who remain are the ones with their own hand, with that very creativity, who can invent

<!-- Same source. -->

### Work you love matters more than wealth

Young people measure the value of work not only in money. This keeps the DS world attractive even when it promises no stability.

> Work you love matters more than wealth, and it's time to accept that too

<!-- Same source, section «What young people want». -->

## What you can do now

1. Separate in your work what AI can do from what only you can do — and invest in the second.
2. Set up a direct channel to those who value your work: a subscription, a newsletter, a club.
3. Agree with two or three colleagues to cover for each other in case of illness or downtime.
4. If you work through an intermediary, check what it is responsible for: finding orders, or trust and quality.

## Transformational Economy in other worlds

The worlds differ in who decides what things are worth:

- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-cs|CS]] — the algorithm.** Price, rating and asset value.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-ce|CE]] — the system.** What counts as enough and what to protect.
- **DS — you yourself.** Your hand, your name and a direct link to the buyer.
- **[[en/materials/macrotrends/transformational-economy-de|DE]] — your own people.** Exchange, gifts and a life that is "enough".
