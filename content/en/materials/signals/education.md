This overview brings together the 40 signals from the final “Education and Knowledge” game deck and the related discussions from three games. Established educational practices sit alongside standards, research proposals and speculative projects. Each example identifies its type: a vision of a possible future is not treated as a report of implementation.

The signals help explore four questions: what can be delegated to a system; who determines a recognised result; what a person decides for themselves; and what can only be discovered through action and feedback. The “Scenario” field shows our current reading of the example, while “Why this scenario” explains it in plain language. Where the classification has been revised, the earlier deck assignment is recorded separately. Game readings retain their discussion context: one example can help explore different questions.

Descriptions draw on the projects’ sources. “Why this is a signal” and the macrotrend classifications are our analytical interpretations. Discussions are presented without participant names or private stories, while retaining hypotheses and objections. Discussing an example does not imply agreement across the group or independent confirmation of its effectiveness.

Overview date: 12 November 2026. Dates attached to signals concern the specified project, event or publication. “N.d.” means the material used does not support a reliable date. The year of a scenario world is stated separately and is not treated as an implementation date.

## SIG-EDU-001

**Future Chinese Preschool: a virtual world with ongoing progress assessment**

**In brief:** A digital environment combines a lesson scenario with progress assessment.

**Date / period:** 2024; a vision of education in 2035.  
**Geography:** United Kingdom; a project about China.  
**Type and stage:** Speculative RCA graduation project.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** The lesson plan, digital environment and progress tracking are combined in a system designed in advance. Part of organising and assessing learning is delegated to that arrangement.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-002 — Human–machine interaction](#mt-002); [MT-047 — Advanced AI systems and cognitive computing](#mt-047).

**Life domains:** Education; Close relationships and family.  
**Industries:** Education; EdTech; VR.  
**Thematic tags:** `#VR` `#preschool` `#progress-assessment`.

### Description

Future Chinese Preschool is a speculative project about Chinese preschool education with a 2035 horizon. Its subject is animal welfare. A virtual game connects to a teacher's lesson plan and a children's activity handbook, extending the topic into practical activities after VR.

One fictional world reverses familiar power relations: pandas lose their habitats, dominate humans and introduce “bamboo credits.” This invites a different perspective on human–animal relationships. Teachers guide the course and monitor each child's weekly progress. The proposal combines a digital story, off-screen activities and assessment within one learning system; the project description does not establish its implementation in preschools.

### Why this is a signal

Teaching may become more closely connected to digital lesson scenarios and accumulated data about a child. The question is which decisions remain with the teacher.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Synthetic.** The possible preschool was read as a way to reduce lesson preparation and provide more regular feedback on a child’s progress. The question was which decisions a system could support and where pedagogical judgement was needed. This is a reading of a design project, not a report of widespread practice in Chinese preschools.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-B-011. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

This is a proposal for a possible learning environment. The existence of the project does not establish the effectiveness of its VR activities or assessment mechanism.

## SIG-EDU-002

**Rethinking the Future of Education: reconfiguring teacher and student roles around AI**

**In brief:** A visual story about a school in 2035 invites teachers and students to explore what happens to learning when instant access to information suddenly disappears. Through this scenario, the project questions the role of AI, independent thinking and human connection in education.

**Date / period:** 2025 — project; 2035 — the setting of the fictional story.  
**Geography:** United Kingdom.  
**Type and stage:** Speculative RCA graduation project.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** Routine tasks and the provision of explanations are proposed as functions for AI. The project asks what can be entrusted to a system and which decisions, abilities and relationships should remain with teachers and learners.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-047 — Advanced AI systems and cognitive computing](#mt-047); [MT-002 — Human–machine interaction](#mt-002).

**Life domains:** Education; Work.  
**Industries:** Education; EdTech; service design.  
**Thematic tags:** `#AI` `#teacher-role` `#learning-environment` `#critical-thinking` `#empathy` `#speculative-design`.

### Description

A speculative RCA project explores the relationships between teachers, students and learning in a world where AI becomes a familiar part of education. The poster identifies two connected concerns. A quick AI answer may substitute for deeper inquiry, understanding and independent reasoning. At the same time, the teacher’s position changes: when a personalised explanation is available on demand, delivering information no longer exhausts the role. These are the project’s research questions, not evidence that every use of AI harms thinking.

The preferred future centres on critical thinking, empathy and the teacher–student relationship. AI handles routine tasks, freeing time for teaching and emotional support. Teachers help learners make sense of material, learn alongside them and encourage deeper discussion. Students use AI ethically and responsibly, while educators participate in shaping the rules for its use.

### How the intervention works

The project created a visual storybook, **Adrift in the Cloud**, for this shared discussion. It is set in 2035, when AI is already embedded in education. A sudden blackout deprives the characters of instant access to information. Teachers and students are invited to consider how they would respond and adapt.

Readers encounter the story, reflect on its scenarios and relate them to their own educational practice. On the poster, this journey connects to visiting a website, writing a reflection, reading other educators’ responses and joining an online community. The book is intended to prompt a substantive conversation about what people delegate to technology and which abilities and relationships they want to retain themselves.

### A pathway towards change

The project uses a futures cone to distinguish probable from preferable futures, and backcasting to work backwards from a desired outcome. Its proposed pathway includes awareness and reflection, building a teacher community, shifts in mindset, and later involvement in education policy and guideline development. The project’s immediate work focuses on the first two steps: making sense of the situation and creating a community. The poster notes that an individual teacher may understand AI risks while feeling unable to address them alone.

### Why this is a signal

The signal highlights a change in education’s task when an answer is instantly available: evaluating that answer, connecting it to context, continuing an inquiry and sustaining dialogue become important. The proposed combination of a future story, reflection and community is intended to involve teachers and students in discussing this change.

Its connection to the original Centralized — Synthetic scenario remains the delegation of routine functions to AI and a change in access to explanations. The blackout story also raises a boundary question: what should teachers and students understand and be able to do independently when a familiar digital assistant is unavailable? Human connection, critical judgement and responsibility become matters of deliberate choice in designing learning.

### Limits of the conclusion

The year 2035 and the book’s blackout are fictional conditions for discussion, not a prediction of an event. The poster describes speculative design and a proposed form of engagement; it does not establish the effectiveness of the book or community for developing thinking, empathy or learning outcomes. Its concerns about quick AI answers do not show that every use of AI inevitably reduces learner independence. Education policy and systemic change are presented as a further strategy rather than an already achieved result.

## SIG-EDU-003

**Gamolution: changing social rules and exploring the culture that might emerge**

**In brief:** Artificial societies provide a setting for exploring changes to rules.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** Online; artificial societies within a model.  
**Type and stage:** Experimental simulation platform.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** A person sets the rules of an artificial society, and the model shows behaviour that might emerge. The focus is on using a system to investigate and choose rules; applying its results to real life requires separate validation.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-013 — Gamification](#mt-013); [MT-047 — Advanced AI systems and cognitive computing](#mt-047).

**Life domains:** Education; Values, meaning and community.  
**Industries:** Simulation; research into social systems.  
**Thematic tags:** `#simulation` `#rules` `#AI-agents`.

### Description

Gamolution develops simulations of artificial societies. Environmental conditions and agent properties shape interactions from which collective behaviour emerges. The proposed model includes memory, emotion, culture and social ties as computational variables; these do not establish consciousness in its characters.

A visual environment lets researchers change conditions and observe the simulated society's response. This creates a space for comparing possible consequences of different rules. Its educational interest lies in the inquiry process: formulate an assumption, intervene in a model and examine what happens. Applying those results to real people requires separate validation; the simulation alone cannot establish a social forecast.

### Why this is a signal

Simulation may become a means of discussing social and educational rules. Applying its results to a real setting requires separate justification.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Synthetic.** Participants proposed using the simulation of rules to explore how upbringing, education and culture might change. This raised questions about the right to choose rules for others and about applying model results to a living society. The game did not establish Gamolution’s predictive accuracy.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-A-012. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

A simulation shows how a model behaves under particular assumptions. It does not establish that people in a real society would behave in the same way.

## SIG-EDU-004

**Bubble Talk: a personalised museum guide that also takes visitors beyond their familiar interests**

**In brief:** A personal museum route includes topics unfamiliar to the visitor.

**Date / period:** 2026.  
**Geography:** Austria, Linz.  
**Type and stage:** Experimental Ars Electronica Futurelab prototype.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** A system selects the museum route, taking account of a person’s interests and adding unfamiliar topics. Even the move beyond familiar interests is partly organised by a digital guide.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-002 — Human–machine interaction](#mt-002); [MT-047 — Advanced AI systems and cognitive computing](#mt-047).

**Life domains:** Education; Everyday life, consumption and leisure; Values, meaning and community.  
**Industries:** Museums; cultural education; AI interfaces.  
**Thematic tags:** `#museum` `#personalisation` `#curiosity`.

### Description

Bubble Talk is an experimental AI guide by Ars Electronica Futurelab and Toyota Frontier Research Center. It builds a tour around visitors' interests, knowledge and curiosity, linking familiar subjects to unexpected artworks and questions beyond their usual interests.

At Ars Electronica Festival 2026, visitors could test it in the Connected Earth exhibition. They received an explanation and a device, explored independently and completed a short questionnaire; the experience took roughly half an hour. The museum thus becomes both a personalised learning environment and a setting for researching the recommendation process. The project investigates whether personalisation can broaden curiosity as well as respond to existing interests.

### Why this is a signal

Personalisation can include encounters with things people would not have chosen themselves. This raises questions about the purpose and limits of such intervention.

### Game discussions

**Decentralized — Earth.** The personalised route was seen as an opportunity to encounter unexpected material and move beyond familiar interests. The example was read as an expansion of inquiry, although it belongs to Centralized — Synthetic in the original deck: the system still shapes the route. A suggestion was to introduce unfamiliar topics and perspectives into a habitual learning path; the prototype’s effect was not measured.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-A-036. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

The prototype explores the possibility of encouraging curiosity. Its description does not establish lasting changes in a visitor’s knowledge or behaviour.

## SIG-EDU-005

**TUMO Path: an algorithm builds a personal route through areas chosen by the learner**

**In brief:** A person chooses their interests; a system suggests a learning sequence.

**Date / period:** 14 February 2020 — publication; the page also lists a 2021 update.  
**Geography:** Armenia; the international TUMO network.  
**Type and stage:** Documented practice of personalised learning pathways.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** The learner chooses areas of interest, while an algorithm assembles and adjusts the sequence of activities. The organisation of the route and coordination of next steps are delegated to a system.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-002 — Human–machine interaction](#mt-002); [MT-081 — Ecosystems, super-apps and the platform economy](#mt-081).

**Life domains:** Education.  
**Industries:** Supplementary education; EdTech.  
**Thematic tags:** `#learning-pathway` `#choice` `#personalisation`.

### Description

At TUMO, teenagers choose creative and technological areas of interest. TUMO Path turns those choices into a sequence of self-learning activities and instructor-led workshops. Its interface shows how individual activities connect and lead to the next stage.

The route changes with the learner: switching subjects, repeating an activity or rescheduling a workshop prompts the system to rebuild the sequence. Pace reflects individual preferences and progress. Coaches and instructors remain part of the process. The model combines personal choice with an organised route: learners can depart from recommendations while the system helps connect their decisions to available learning opportunities.

### Why this is a signal

Choosing a goal and arranging the route are separated: the learner chooses directions, while the system coordinates steps and scheduling.

### Limits of the conclusion

A personal route does not remove the programme’s boundaries, mentors, workshops or infrastructure. The algorithm does not determine a person’s interests independently of their choices.

## SIG-EDU-006

**Mastery Transcript: layers of evidence of ability replace a single grade**

**In brief:** Competencies and the work supporting them are visible alongside one another.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** United States; international use.  
**Type and stage:** Platform and format for learner records.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** Ability is described through school-defined competencies and verified work. A shared record format lets others examine the result against explicit criteria.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-044 — Social trust and digital ethics](#mt-044); [MT-031 — Human mastery](#mt-031).

**Life domains:** Education; Work.  
**Industries:** School education; assessment; admissions.  
**Thematic tags:** `#portfolio` `#criteria` `#evidence-of-skills`.

### Description

Mastery Transcript is an interactive school record connecting competencies to evidence. Learners submit projects, performances or other work; educators review it against school criteria and may request revisions. A competency enters the record once the evidence meets the standard.

Readers first see a compact profile, then open the work supporting an achievement. Schools define their own competencies while a shared format makes records readable for admissions staff and employers. Two products serve different purposes: the Mastery Transcript replaces a conventional graded transcript, while the MTC Learning Record can accompany one. Learner choice about featured work sits alongside the school's responsibility for the official record.

### Why this is a signal

Evidence of ability becomes multilayered. Recognition of the result depends on criteria and the receiving organisation’s decision.

### Game discussions

In the first two recordings, the caption about evidence of work appeared briefly in an overview of examples.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-A-005; OBS-GAME-EDU-B-005. -->

**Sorting examples between scenarios.** The format later became a subject of disagreement: was it personal diagnosis, a new common standard, or a means of feedback? The discussion’s conclusion was that a portfolio does not itself determine who sets the criteria or who recognises the result. It helps to first identify the purpose of assessment and the person or organisation that will use it.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-C-003. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

A portfolio format does not guarantee identical recognition by every institution. Criteria and the quality of evidence still matter.

## SIG-EDU-007

**Open Badges 3.0: a skill becomes a portable, verifiable record**

**In brief:** An achievement is linked to its issuer, criteria and evidence.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** International standard.  
**Type and stage:** Specification for digital achievement credentials.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** An achievement is recorded through a common standard, identifying its issuer, criteria and evidence. The authenticity of the record can be checked through an established digital procedure.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-044 — Social trust and digital ethics](#mt-044); [MT-066 — Transformation of work and talent management](#mt-066).

**Life domains:** Education; Work.  
**Industries:** Education; HR; digital credentials.  
**Thematic tags:** `#microcredentials` `#Open-Badges` `#verifiable-record`.

### Description

Open Badges is a standard for recording a specific achievement, such as a skill, programme or microcredential. A badge identifies its recipient, issuer, achievement and criteria, and may include evidence of completed work. It can move between compatible systems and appear alongside a résumé or portfolio.

Version 3.0 uses verifiable credentials and digital signatures. A recipient can check who issued a record and whether it has changed, distinguishing a badge image from a verifiable learning claim. The issuer remains responsible for the criteria and significance of the achievement: technical authenticity alone does not make different badges equivalent qualifications.

### Why this is a signal

Evidence of a skill can move between systems. Verifying a signature and recognising a professional qualification remain different decisions.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Synthetic.** Achievement, criteria and evidence were compared with a new kind of learning record. A distinction emerged between a record’s portability and its recognition: a digital badge can be verified, yet organisations may assess its content differently. A universal professional licence does not follow from the standard.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-B-013. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

The authenticity of a digital record does not establish learning quality or universal eligibility to practise a profession.

## SIG-EDU-008

**Comprehensive Learner Record: learning and work form one verifiable digital history**

**In brief:** Achievements from different settings form a portable learning history.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** International standard.  
**Type and stage:** Specification for interoperable digital learning histories.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** Different achievements are assembled in an interoperable, verifiable format. This reading focuses on a shared way of recording and checking experience that educators and employers can use.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-044 — Social trust and digital ethics](#mt-044); [MT-066 — Transformation of work and talent management](#mt-066).

**Life domains:** Education; Work; Values, meaning and community.  
**Industries:** Education; HR; digital credentials.  
**Thematic tags:** `#CLR` `#portfolio` `#privacy` `#informal-learning`.

### Description

A Comprehensive Learner Record is a standard for combining achievements in a machine-readable, verifiable record. It can include course outcomes, skills, internships, badges and workplace learning. Achievements remain associated with the organisations certifying them.

A person may hold several CLRs from different organisations and present them when continuing education or seeking work. A compatible format helps recipients read and verify the achievements. Whereas a badge usually represents one achievement, a CLR bundles many into a broader learning history. The model supports learner control over sharing; it does not require a single registry automatically collecting a person's entire life.

### Why this is a signal

Learning histories extend beyond a single institution. The ability to demonstrate experience also raises questions about data disclosure and the consequences of assessment.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Synthetic.** A digital history of learning and work was considered as a possible infrastructure for control: who gets access, what can they see about a person, and can an incorrect profile be revised? Surveillance of an entire life is a scenario proposed in the game, not a required CLR function.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-A-011. -->

**Centralized — Synthetic.** Another reading was to make experience visible that previously did not enter a portfolio. The discussion covered the cost of gathering evidence, criteria for ability and the move from assessment to decisions about eligibility. Participation, acquired ability and recognition by an organisation should be distinguished. Not every experience needs to become data; the game did not develop a finished system for protection or appeals. The next-step question was what evidence a particular goal requires, who will see it and which decision it will improve.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-B-010; OBS-GAME-EDU-B-020; OBS-GAME-EDU-B-024; OBS-GAME-EDU-B-025; OBS-GAME-EDU-B-028. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

The standard does not require the collection of an entire private life, nor does it create state surveillance by itself. Data interoperability and recognition of qualifications are different matters.

## SIG-EDU-009

**An Automatic Workshop: trying to run a session without a facilitator present**

**In brief:** Part of the facilitator’s work is embedded in instructions available to the group.

**Date / period:** 2018 — the workshop documented in the publication.  
**Geography:** Netherlands, Amsterdam.  
**Type and stage:** Documented experiment with group instructions.  
**Scenario:** CS — Centralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** Part of the facilitator’s work is written into instructions and a sequence of actions. The group can follow the session using a process arranged in advance.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy](#mt-043); [MT-007 — Work automation](#mt-007); [MT-031 — Human mastery](#mt-031).

**Life domains:** Education.  
**Industries:** Informal education; facilitation; design.  
**Thematic tags:** `#instructions` `#workshop` `#self-organisation`.

### Description

An Automatic Workshop was an exercise at the 2018 Hackers & Designers summer academy. Participants imagined a workshop as instructions executable without its facilitator. The exercise itself arrived in that form: a script and material kit intended to explain the group's task.

Preparing it required making explicit the actions and transitions a facilitator normally explains or improvises. The authors anticipated unexpected situations while preserving room for interpretation. The experiment examines how learning is organised: what instructions can carry, and where a group must negotiate its next action. It explores a workshop format rather than establishing that scripts can universally replace teachers.

### Why this is a signal
