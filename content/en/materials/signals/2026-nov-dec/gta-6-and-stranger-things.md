*In short. In GTA 6 the game is a mirror: it shows what people lack in reality but builds no bridge into life. In Stranger Things the game is a bridge: the kids carry the language of Dungeons & Dragons into reality and use it to understand and defeat the unknown. Neither story changes the system. But in GTA salvation lies in the couple, and in Stranger Things in the community. And the dungeon master in D&D shows how a centre can use a game without turning it into propaganda: set the frame, but not dictate the answer.*

This continues the note [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/gta-6-life-in-the-game|"Games are not an escape but proof that reality is badly designed"]].

## A game inside a story

GTA 6 is itself a game, while in [[en/media-library/films/stranger-things|Stranger Things]] the game is part of the plot. The Duffer brothers' series opens in 1983 with a game of Dungeons & Dragons in Mike Wheeler's basement and closes with one too: the finale came out on 31 December 2025. The creators say they planned this full circle from the start.

D&D is a tabletop role-playing game. The dungeon master sets the rules, and the players decide how their characters act. Nobody knows the outcome in advance, the master included.

## Stranger Things: the game as a language for the unknown

When inexplicable things start happening in Hawkins, the kids name them with words from the game. The monster becomes the Demogorgon, the hive mind of the parallel world the Mind Flayer, the main villain Vecna. These are not just references: the game gives them a ready model of reality. Who the enemy is, what its rules and weaknesses are, what role each person plays on the team, what to do next.

Our reading: this is pure sensemaking. To name the unknown is to make it manageable. Here the game is not an escape from a frightening reality but a tool for entering it.

There is a subtle detail that reviewers of the finale noticed. The rescued younger children name their fears not with D&D words but with images from Madeleine L'Engle's A Wrinkle in Time. So it is not about a particular game: any story a person knows can become a language for what is happening to them. What matters is having such a language.

The final scene: eighteen months after the events, the friends play one last game before leaving for college. Mike runs a campaign in which you can recognise the story of their own adventures. Then one by one they put away their books and go upstairs, and Mike's younger sister Holly and her friends rush down into the basement to start their own campaign. Holly was added to the finale later, when the creators realised how important she had become in the last season. The tool is handed to the next generation.

## Where the system is

Stranger Things has two centralised systems.

- **Hawkins Lab.** A state that experiments on children, including Eleven, and uses people as a resource. In the last season the town ends up under military control.
- **The Upside Down.** A parallel world with a hive mind where everything is subject to one will. In essence, total centralisation in the shape of a monster.

Against them stands a horizontal network, pure [[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|DE, the community world]]: teenagers, their families, a few lone adults, and Eleven, who escaped from the lab. No organisation, hierarchy or resources, only relationships and a shared language.

How it ends: Vecna and the Upside Down are defeated, but the system does not go anywhere. A telling detail: Eleven disappears so the others can live normal lives instead of hiding her. There is still no freedom in this world: a heroine the system might hunt has to leave to protect her people. Mike, however, believes she is alive.

## Comparison

| | GTA 6 | Stranger Things |
|---|---|---|
| Where the game is | The game is the product | The game is part of the plot |
| Role of the game | Mirror: shows what is missing | Bridge: a language for understanding reality |
| Against the system | A couple | A horizontal community |
| The system | An environment: money, media, police, social media | The lab, the military and the Upside Down |
| How it ends | Prison or death, the world unchanged | The monster defeated, power remains |
| Bridge into life | None, the exit is up to the player | Yes, it is the plot itself |
| Who passes the game on | Nobody, everyone plays alone | The older kids give the basement to the younger |

What the two stories share: neither shows how to change the system. Both show how to live and protect your people inside it. This matches the mass image of the future: the system is stronger than the individual and will not change, but in your relationships and decisions you can do a lot.

The difference is where to look for strength. GTA says: in the couple. Stranger Things says: in a community with a shared language.

## The dungeon master: can a centre use games?

Talking about GTA, we arrived at the rules under which people enjoy solving problems: the problem is chosen by yourself, it is within reach, it has a visible result and failure is safe. A conclusion suggests itself: games are a tool of the decentralised world, and systems cannot use them, because voluntary participation disappears and the game turns into propaganda.

That is half true. Philosopher Bernard Suits defined a game as a voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles. Impose a game and it becomes work in a game wrapper. This has been tested in practice: Ethan Mollick and Nancy Rothbard studied gamification in sales (Mandatory Fun, 2014). The game made work more enjoyable only for those who agreed to it. For those who were dragged into it, the effect was negative.

But systems do use games, and successfully.

- **Corporations**, for attention and money. GTA itself is made by a giant centralised company, and GTA Online earned from in-game purchases for years. Apps with points, streaks and loot boxes are gamification too, in the service of retention.
- **States**, for ideology. Since 2002 the US Army has released the game America's Army for recruitment, and China has the Xuexi Qiangguo app, which awards points for studying Party materials. This is propaganda in game form, and it works where coercion stands behind it.

And the games industry itself is not decentralised: online games mean servers, platform rules, bans and controlled economies. What is decentralised is the player's experience, not ownership.

So the line runs not between system and person but along four criteria:

1. **Free entry and exit.** You can choose not to play and leave without loss.
2. **Transparent rules.** The player sees how the system works and is not nudged covertly.
3. **Open outcome.** The game has no "right answer" it steers you towards.
4. **The player's goal.** The game serves the goal of the person playing, not of the person who made it.

A game becomes propaganda when the third criterion is broken. It becomes manipulation when the second and fourth are broken.

The dungeon master in D&D is an example of a centre that meets all four. He sets the world and the rules, so he is a centre. But the rules are open, the players came of their own accord, the outcome is not predetermined, and the master does not lead them to the right answer but responds to their decisions. The commercial game released for the finale works the same way: the Welcome to the Hellfire Club set invites you to gather the gang and choose how the story ends.

Hence the formula: **a game works as long as it has no right answer.** Once it gets one, whether ideology, a purchase or holding attention, the game turns into a tool of influence and loses the main thing: a choice that belongs to the player.

## What this means for people who make games about the future

Stranger Things shows three conditions under which a game becomes a bridge and not just a mirror.

1. **Language.** The game provides words and models for what has no name yet.
2. **Group.** It is played together, and the shared language becomes the basis for joint action.
3. **Transfer.** There is a moment when the frame from the game is applied to a real situation.

GTA gives the player an incredibly rich experience but meets none of these conditions: people play alone, the language stays inside, there is no transfer. Scenario games, including Decoder games, are built like Mike's basement: a group, a shared language of scenarios and signals, a facilitator in the dungeon master's role who sets the world but does not dictate the answer. And the transfer everything is for: participants take the frame into their own lives and work.

## Questions and answers

**What role does Dungeons & Dragons play in Stranger Things?**
It is the language the kids use to describe the inexplicable: the Demogorgon, the Mind Flayer and Vecna are named after creatures from the game. The game gives them a model of reality and assigns roles on the team. The series begins and ends with a game of D&D.

**How do GTA 6 and Stranger Things differ in how they treat games?**
In GTA 6 the game is a mirror: it shows what people lack in life but does not carry them into it. In Stranger Things the game is a bridge: its language helps the characters understand reality and act.

**Do the characters change the system in Stranger Things?**
No. They defeat the monster, but power remains. Eleven even disappears so the others can live normally. Like GTA, the series shows how to protect your people inside the system, but the strength lies in community rather than in a couple.

**Can a state or a company use games?**
It can, but a game stays a game only under four conditions: free entry and exit, transparent rules, an open outcome and the player's own goal. If the game has a predetermined "right answer", it turns into propaganda or manipulation.

**How does Bernard Suits define a game?**
As a voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles. Without voluntariness there is no game.
