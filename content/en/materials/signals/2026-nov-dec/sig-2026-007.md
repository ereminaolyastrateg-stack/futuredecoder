*SIG-2026-007 · Canaries in the coal mine*

**In brief:** ADP payroll data through June 2026 show that employment of young workers in highly AI-exposed occupations is about 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with their peers.

**Geography:** USA.

**Collection trend:** [[en/materials/signals/2026-november-december#trend-2|2. The entrance to work is closing]] — AI is not yet destroying professions, but it is changing who gets hired into them. The main fear is not a machine uprising but becoming unnecessary.

### Description

ADP payroll data through June 2026 show that employment of young workers in highly AI-exposed occupations is about 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with their peers. The decline comes through reduced hiring; where AI complements rather than replaces, employment of experienced workers is flat or rising.

### Why this is a signal

The first rung of the career ladder disappears before the professions themselves.

### Where it came up

Stanford Digital Economy Lab, “Canaries in the Coal Mine?”, August 2026 revision.

### Limits of the conclusion

The authors describe their findings as early descriptive indicators rather than causal estimates.
