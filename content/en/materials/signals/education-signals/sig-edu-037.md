*SIG-EDU-037 · CERN Challenge-Based Innovation*

**In brief:** An interdisciplinary team connects a social problem with research technologies.

**Date / period:** Since 2014 — CBI history in the IdeaSquare report for 2021–2023.  
**Geography:** Switzerland, CERN; international university teams.  
**Type and stage:** Challenge-Based Innovation educational courses.  
**Scenario:** DE — Decentralized — Earth.

**Why this scenario:** A team begins with an open social problem, clarifies the need and makes prototypes. The response and the knowledge required become clearer through inquiry and testing ideas.

**Macrotrend drivers:** MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy; MT-031 — Human mastery; MT-054 — Designing futures and strategic vision.

**Life domains:** Education; Work.  
**Industries:** Engineering education; R&D; social innovation.  
**Thematic tags:** open problem, deep tech, prototype.

### Description

Challenge-Based Innovation connects university teams with IdeaSquare, CERN's open-innovation lab. Students from different disciplines investigate societal challenges, drawing inspiration from CERN science and exchanging with experts. Universities co-create programmes, so content, methods and duration vary between editions.

Teams develop projects, visit IdeaSquare, join discussions and use prototyping facilities, including electronics and 3D-printing workshops. Meetings between university groups enable exchange. An open challenge requires clarifying whose needs a solution serves and giving ideas a testable form. A prototype is an educational research outcome; its suitability for wider use requires further validation.

### Why this is a signal

Learning can begin with an open problem. Students need to clarify the need, connect knowledge and test a response themselves.

### Limits of the conclusion

An open problem requires inquiry and support. Access to technology does not guarantee a viable solution.
