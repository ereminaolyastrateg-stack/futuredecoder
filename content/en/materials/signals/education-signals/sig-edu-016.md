*SIG-EDU-016 · Solar Mamas*

**In brief:** Women from rural communities learn to maintain solar systems.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** India; rural communities in the Global South.  
**Type and stage:** Practical training programme by Barefoot College.  
**Scenario:** CE — Centralized — Earth.

**Why this scenario:** Training responds to a specific community need: servicing solar systems. Acquired skill gains meaning through a useful technical role and recognition of that contribution.

**Macrotrend drivers:** MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy; MT-031 — Human mastery; MT-038 — Inequality and resource distribution.

**Life domains:** Education; Work; Place of living and public environment.  
**Industries:** Solar energy; vocational learning.  
**Thematic tags:** practice, access to learning, solar energy.

### Description

Solar Mamas is a Barefoot College programme for women from remote rural communities, including those with limited formal literacy. Six months of intensive residential practical training cover making, installing and repairing solar lanterns and home-lighting systems. Learning addresses a specific need in villages without reliable electricity.

On returning, participants receive equipment and spare parts to establish local electronic workshops. They can assemble, install and maintain systems and share knowledge with neighbours. Education therefore includes both individual preparation and the means to continue working within a community. “Solar engineers” describes their programme role; the source does not establish equivalence to a university engineering qualification.

### Why this is a signal

A route into a useful technical role can be built through practical training and access to equipment, taking account of a particular community’s conditions.

### Game discussions

**Decentralized — Earth.** Practical training was seen as a way to recognise ability outside familiar school-based eligibility requirements. Formal literacy, an acquired skill and recognition of a contribution need to be distinguished.

**Centralized — Earth.** Another reading foregrounded the needs of a place: depth of preparation is related to a community’s specific technical task. A practical role within the programme is not a universal engineering qualification. Limited literacy is not an advantage in itself; theory and support do not become unnecessary.

### Limits of the conclusion

The programme teaches a defined set of tasks involving solar systems. This is not equivalent to every form of regulated engineering education.
