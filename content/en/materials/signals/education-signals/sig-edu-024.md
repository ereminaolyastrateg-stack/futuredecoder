*SIG-EDU-024 · superFUTURES*

**In brief:** Possible worlds are explored through space, stories and film.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** United Kingdom, RCA.  
**Type and stage:** Research and educational unit.  
**Scenario:** DS — Decentralized — Synthetic.

**Why this scenario:** A person can construct several images of the future and consider which resonate with them and which assumptions sustain them. The emphasis is on their own imagination and relationship to possible futures.

**Macrotrend drivers:** MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy; MT-054 — Designing futures and strategic vision; MT-030 — Culture as a source of futures.

**Life domains:** Education; Values, meaning and community.  
**Industries:** Design education; foresight; film.  
**Thematic tags:** #theme/imagination #theme/scenarios #theme/world-building

### Description

superFUTURES is an RCA research-led unit investigating possible ways of living through space, scenarios and film. Students use foresight, storytelling and world-building to explore changing relationships with environments, technologies and other species.

Interior space becomes a research medium that makes a scenario's rules, habits and opportunities visible. Film shows the imagined world in action and brings it closer to viewers' experience. The unit uses feminist and queer perspectives to question established design norms. Its work generates multiple possible worlds for discussion and inquiry. Ficta Ordo and Nature's Depot, elsewhere in this overview, are examples from the same unit.

### Why this is a signal

Imagination is treated as a practice for exploring assumptions and alternatives, rather than finding a single correct forecast.

### Game discussions

**Decentralized — Synthetic.** The discussion covered imagination, several images of the future and the ability to tell their stories. Different worlds help reveal assumptions and alternatives for action. Multiple scenarios do not make facts arbitrary; it is useful to state the conditions under which each possibility could occur.

### Limits of the conclusion

A scenario helps explore what is possible. A persuasive form does not turn it into an established fact.
