*SIG-EDU-019 · Design Factory Global Network*

**In brief:** University sites form a network for collaborative project work.

**Date / period:** n.d.  
**Geography:** International network; coordination in Finland.  
**Type and stage:** Network of educational innovation hubs.  
**Scenario:** [[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|CE — Centralized — Earth]].

**Why this scenario:** Universities and partners establish a shared setting and ways of working for educational projects. A challenge meaningful to the network provides direction, while ability is demonstrated through collaborative practical work.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy-ce|MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy]]; [[en/materials/macrotrends/human-mastery-ce|MT-031 — Human mastery]]; MT-066 — Transformation of work and talent management.

**Life domains:** Education; Work.  
**Industries:** Higher education; R&D; industry collaboration.  
**Thematic tags:** Design Factory, interdisciplinarity, prototype.

### Description

Design Factory Global Network connects university and research sites where students, educators and external partners work on practical challenges. It supports exchange and international collaboration, while local centres determine their courses and specialisms.

For example, HAMK Design Factory describes interdisciplinary industry projects in which students co-create and test solutions and prototypes for real challenges. Other sites focus on digital products and user experience. A shared principle connects learning, development and collaboration with partners in one environment. The university lab becomes a meeting place for professions and interests; network membership does not imply identical curricula or qualifications across its centres.

### Why this is a signal

Learning connects organisations, cultures and disciplines around a real task. Network coordination becomes part of the educational environment.

### Limits of the conclusion

A shared approach does not make every site identical. Programme arrangements and relationships with partners need to be considered in each case.
