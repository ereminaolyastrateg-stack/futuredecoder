*SIG-EDU-031 · Adaptive Neighbourhoods Lab*

**In brief:** A neighbourhood is imagined as a setting for shared trials and revised decisions.

**Date / period:** 2026 — exhibition model; 2070 — the Pluriversity scenario horizon.  
**Geography:** Ireland — the imagined setting; Netherlands — the DDW exhibition.  
**Type and stage:** Speculative lab within The Pluriversity project.  
**Scenario:** DE — Decentralized — Earth.

**Why this scenario:** Residents try ways of living together, observe consequences and revise rules and decisions. Knowledge of a suitable neighbourhood arrangement emerges through repeated trials and feedback.

**Macrotrend drivers:** MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy; MT-054 — Designing futures and strategic vision; MT-029 — Crisis of social institutions.

**Life domains:** Education; Place of living and public environment; Values, meaning and community.  
**Industries:** Territorial development; housing; education.  
**Thematic tags:** #theme/neighbourhood #theme/experiment #theme/feedback

### Description

Adaptive Neighbourhoods Lab belongs to The Pluriversity's fictional world in a future Limerick Docklands. Homes can be disassembled and reconfigured, tools and building elements are shared, and a communal garden connects food growing, water management and gathering.

Learning is embedded in this life. Proposed cycles investigate shared wellbeing, imagine neighbourhood futures and prototype adaptable spaces. Contributions include residents' stories, shared-use agreements and material prototypes. Some ideas remain; others are revised or undone. The project illustrates continuing learning through the consequences of collective decisions. It is a lab in a speculative university model for 2070, not an account of an operating neighbourhood.

### Why this is a signal

Knowledge about shared life may accumulate as a history of tested and revised decisions. This belongs to the same speculative project as SIG-EDU-011.

### Limits of the conclusion

This is part of the speculative Pluriversity world, not independent evidence of an operating neighbourhood-university.
