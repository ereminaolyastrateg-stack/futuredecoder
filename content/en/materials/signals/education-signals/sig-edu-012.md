*SIG-EDU-012 · Regenerative Future(s)*

**In brief:** The purpose of training designers is reconsidered through restoration of the environment.

**Date / period:** 26 March 2025 — online publication.  
**Geography:** United Kingdom — RCA research.  
**Type and stage:** Research chapter and proposed educational framework.  
**Scenario:** [[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|CE — Centralized — Earth]].

**Why this scenario:** A common purpose is proposed for designers: restoring ecological and social conditions. It defines what counts as valuable and responsible professional work.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy-ce|MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy]]; MT-029 — Crisis of social institutions; MT-054 — Designing futures and strategic vision.

**Life domains:** [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/education-ce|Education]]; [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/work-ce|Work]]; [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/meaning-ce|Values, meaning and community]].  
**Industries:** Design education; ecological design.  
**Thematic tags:** design, regeneration, responsibility.

### Description

Regenerative Future(s) examines what future designers should learn and what their work should serve. It considers the limits of industrial design amid climate disruption and proposes ecological and social values as foundations for professional decisions, with restoration of living conditions as an educational purpose.

The paper connects examples of RCA doctoral research with ecological critique, systems thinking and futures studies to propose an educational framework. This reframes the learning question: how does design affect living systems and social justice? The publication sets out a direction for education; it does not document a unified curriculum already implemented across the RCA.

### Why this is a signal

The criteria for a good project may include its ecological and social consequences. This changes the grounds on which professional work is recognised.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Synthetic: a game reading.** Participants proposed making a shift towards ecological and social values visible by counting projects. This raises an assessment question: the number of declared projects is not the same as their ecological effect, a change in values or the quality of training. The original framework remains a research proposal.

### Limits of the conclusion
