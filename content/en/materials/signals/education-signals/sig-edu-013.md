*SIG-EDU-013 · Learning to Disrupt*

**In brief:** Cultural spaces are considered places for democratic learning.

**Date / period:** 5 September 2025 — conference session.  
**Geography:** Austria, Linz; European practices.  
**Type and stage:** Conference programme on non-formal education.  
**Scenario:** [[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|CE — Centralized — Earth]].

**Why this scenario:** Museums and workshops are proposed for recognition as meaningful settings for democratic education. The shared social question is which places and forms of participation count as education.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy-ce|MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy]]; MT-030 — Culture as a source of futures; MT-029 — Crisis of social institutions.

**Life domains:** [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/education-ce|Education]]; [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/place-ce|Place of living and public environment]]; [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/meaning-ce|Values, meaning and community]].  
**Industries:** Museums; cultural institutions; non-formal education.  
**Thematic tags:** museum, democracy, non formal education.

### Description

Learning to Disrupt was an Ars Electronica 2025 conference session about democratic education beyond formal schooling. It considered museums, makerspaces and cultural organisations as places where young people can investigate public problems and develop their own responses. Examples in its framing included co-designed climate labs and narrative workshops on care and justice.

Youth participation meant opportunities to question, propose alternatives and shape the work. Funding, partnerships and recognition in education policy were also central concerns. The signal records a public discussion about the role and support of non-formal education; a conference programme alone does not establish the outcomes of every practice it mentions.

### Why this is a signal

The educational role of a museum or workshop expands to include inquiry into the rules of public life and participation in changing them.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Earth.** Museums and workshops were considered places to question the established system and learn in other ways. Three changes should be separated: where people learn, how they acquire knowledge and who recognises the result. An expanded learning environment does not imply the disappearance of conventional institutions.
