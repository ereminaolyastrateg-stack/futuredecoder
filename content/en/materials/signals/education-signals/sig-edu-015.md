*SIG-EDU-015 · Learning Planet Academy*

**In brief:** The university’s mission is connected to shared planetary challenges.

**Date / period:** January 2026 — the academy launch listed on its page.  
**Geography:** France; international programmes.  
**Type and stage:** Educational initiative and manifesto for a university mission.  
**Scenario:** [[en/four-worlds/centralized-earth|CE — Centralized — Earth]].

**Why this scenario:** A common direction is proposed for the university: serving the world and addressing planetary challenges. This mission gives training its purpose and defines a publicly useful result.

**Macrotrend drivers:** [[en/materials/macrotrends/education-knowledge-literacy-ce|MT-043 — Education, knowledge and literacy]]; MT-029 — Crisis of social institutions; MT-054 — Designing futures and strategic vision.

**Life domains:** [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/education-ce|Education]]; [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/work-ce|Work]]; [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/meaning-ce|Values, meaning and community]].  
**Industries:** Higher education; social and environmental projects.  
**Thematic tags:** university mission, public benefit, interdisciplinarity.

### Description

Learning Planet Academy brings educational initiatives together around climate, peace and democracy, mental health and wellbeing. Its model combines project learning, research, experimentation and collaboration. Young people are invited to help design and govern learning formats.

Its manifesto proposes a university connecting people and knowledge to care for shared resources. Stated principles include transdisciplinarity, social entrepreneurship and AI-supported personalisation. Offerings include youth project programmes, seasonal schools and research activities. The academy provides a common mission and a collection of initiatives, so learning conditions and recognition of outcomes need to be understood programme by programme rather than assumed across every format.

### Why this is a signal

The university describes itself as a setting for joint action. The question for such a mission is how to distinguish a promise of benefit from an observable result.

### Limits of the conclusion

A mission and an educational initiative are not evidence that the stated planetary challenges have been solved.
