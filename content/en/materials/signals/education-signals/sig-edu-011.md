*SIG-EDU-011 · The Pluriversity*

**In brief:** The university is imagined as a network of labs organised around the needs of a place.

**Date / period:** 2026 — exhibition project; 2070 — scenario horizon.  
**Geography:** Ireland — the imagined setting; Netherlands — the DDW exhibition.  
**Type and stage:** Speculative university model by Studio Saol.

**Why this scenario:** In the proposed model, learning follows the needs of a living bioregion: people try responses, receive feedback from the place and its communities, and revise their actions. The university evolves with what is discovered through that process.

### Description

The Pluriversity is Studio Saol's speculative model presented at Dutch Design Week 2026. Visitors encounter a university as prospective learners in 2070. A network of labs serves specific missions in the Shannon bioregion and its communities.

The installation gives each lab a mission, learner's story, future artefact and learning cycles to join. The project website describes lifelong learning, mentorship, tolerance of failure and distributed governance. In this reading, education continues as the territory changes: new circumstances call for new knowledge and action. The exhibition presents a possible institutional design whose implementation remains an open question.

### Why this is a signal

The value of education is connected to the life of a territory and its community. The project explores a different university mission, not only a different teaching method.

### Game discussions

**Centralized — Synthetic: placement in the exercise.** A university organised around a bioregion’s tasks prompted discussion of learning outside school, connections to place and the ability to shape the educational environment. Placement in the Centralized — Synthetic exercise did not itself imply automation: the original deck assigned the project to Centralized — Earth.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-A-008; OBS-GAME-EDU-A-030. -->

**Centralized — Synthetic.** Participants imagined a system that observes a region and identifies the specialists it needs, potentially as a digital twin. This system is a game hypothesis. The original project presents a speculative university, not an operating automated workforce infrastructure.

**Possible additional reference.** The third recording includes a discussion of a bioregional learning network without the card’s title being read aloud. It raised a question: who sets the local network’s purpose, and how much freedom does the learner have? The connection of this fragment specifically to Pluriversity remains tentative.

### Limits of the conclusion

2070 is the horizon of a speculative world. This model should not be described as an already operating network of universities.
