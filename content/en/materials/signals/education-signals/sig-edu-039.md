*SIG-EDU-039 · Rightful Presence and Radical Making*

**In brief:** Young people are considered co-authors of the learning environment.

**Date / period:** 5 September 2025 — lecture and discussion.  
**Geography:** Austria, Linz; international makerspace practices.  
**Type and stage:** Public discussion of research and educational practices.  
**Scenario:** [[en/four-worlds/decentralized-earth|DE — Decentralized — Earth]].

**Why this scenario:** Young people help create the space and its rules through collective action and critical inquiry. The organisation of learning becomes something participants can discuss and change as they work.

### Description

Rightful Presence and Radical Making was an Ars Electronica 2025 conversation about power and inequality in technical education. It focused on makerspaces where young people create things and investigate technology, asking what would give them meaningful influence over these environments.

The questions concern choosing tasks, recognising participants' experience and discussing technology's social consequences. Making connects to critical inquiry into who decides and whose interests count. The session frames participation as co-creating an educational environment. This is a discussion topic and research direction; its programme does not establish a universal method or youth control of every makerspace.

### Why this is a signal

Participation can mean an opportunity to discuss and alter rules, rather than only access equipment and complete someone else’s task.

### Game discussions

**Decentralized — Earth.** Young people as co-authors of a makerspace were connected to the right to participate in creating and testing learning rules. The suggestion was to involve learners in discussing the organisation of their own educational environment.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-A-034. -->

**Centralized — Earth.** The emphasis shifted towards critical inquiry into an existing rule and recognition of the learner’s contribution. The right to participate in a norm and the final right to decide are different. Two scenario readings of one example do not make it two independent signals.

<!-- Discussion provenance: OBS-GAME-EDU-B-054. -->

### Limits of the conclusion

The panel asks about sharing power. Participation, access to equipment and the final right to decide need to be distinguished.
