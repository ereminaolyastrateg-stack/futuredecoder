## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Content is matched to hormones, like a medicine for a state of mind.**

- **How it develops:** To stop people leaving, the platform adds interactivity and “usefulness”: collections that supposedly pump up empathy. Next comes a smart ring: content picked to match your hormone levels. “I need to stay awake for two hours”, “calm me down”. The routine is watching films for the emotion you need.
- **The price:** An illusion of usefulness and meaning. Emotion regulation by subscription (as in Black Mirror).
- **Signal:** The number of episodes watched per person is growing; seriality is built so the chain doesn't let go (a game card).
- **Heard at the game:** “Everyone injects themselves, but dopamine withdrawal is hard to overcome”
- **Step for this week:** Before you switch on a series, tell yourself what state you want to get into, and check afterwards.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Experiences through communities, with intrigue and commitment.**

- **How it develops:** Business clubs and communities sell significance and exclusivity: the speaker isn't named in advance, members are taken backstage, to other members' production sites, to meet an actor after the show. An experience manager appears (a person or an app). Membership is for a year, with responsibility.
- **The price:** Exclusivity by subscription stops being exclusive; formats get “wilder and wilder” to give a thrill.
- **Signal:** Business club members take each other to their own production sites, which engages more than lectures (the host's example).
- **Heard at the game:** “Get into the guts of it, go backstage”
- **Step for this week:** Go “backstage” of something you use every day: a factory, a workshop, a theatre after the show.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**A collection of your life: you keep what will later become valuable.**

- **How it develops:** Nostalgia was, is and will be, so people start deliberately keeping things that seem useless today: old devices, books, objects. They remember not through photos but through objects. Collections are also a way to find your people.
- **The price:** Collecting easily becomes “managed exclusivity”: limited releases are made to sell at a higher price.
- **Signal:** Teenagers hunt for old point-and-shoot cameras and earlier-generation iPhones; second-hand they cost hundreds of euros (the host's observation).
- **Heard at the game:** “The story of my life, told through objects”
- **Step for this week:** Pick one thing you were going to throw away and think whether it will be valuable in ten years.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Living it rather than recording it.**

- **How it develops:** Constant filming replaces the experience: on a small screen a gilded cathedral looks like “nothing much”, and a celebration filmed from start to finish isn't remembered. The new routine is one deliberate photo, limited shots, attention to the body and what's around.
- **The price:** It means giving up a habit that everything around you supports.
- **Signal:** The host suggested a rule: “30 frames a day, like a film camera”.
- **Heard at the game:** “The most precious experiences are kept in the heart, not on video”
- **Step for this week:** Have a day when you take no more than ten photos.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Don't rewrite the scenario; play inside it** (Все). If we dislike something in a quadrant and remake it to suit ourselves, we assemble a single scenario again. Better to find the actor who decides there and think from their position, for example as a streaming company's board of directors.

- **Paradox:** We try to defend ourselves everywhere, and see only one version of the future.
- **Experiment for a week:** Take a trend you dislike and describe it from the position of whoever benefits from it.
