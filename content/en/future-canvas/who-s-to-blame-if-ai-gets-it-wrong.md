**Who will be responsible for decisions AI suggests?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Nobody is responsible; the algorithm decides.**

- **How it develops:** The state lacks resources, so it automates collection and redistribution. The corporation or the state is the subject; everyone else is an object. Systems dismiss people by efficiency, and it's unclear how to adjust what you do.
- **The price:** Psychological resilience wobbles. The system is dumb and controls simple things; black markets and stagnation appear.
- **Signal:** Systems dismiss employees based on efficiency metrics.
- **Heard at the game:** “Responsibility lies with the algorithm”
- **Step for this week:** When you get a decision from a system, ask: who exactly stands behind it?

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Passed an AI exam? You're responsible yourself.**

- **How it develops:** AI in clinics sorts requests and makes preliminary diagnoses. Like a car: you're behind the wheel, so it's your fault. The state introduces an exam for using AI: pass it and you take responsibility. Parents take an exam so their child can study with a neural network.
- **The price:** The exam is insurance for the system, not a service. Responsibility is increasingly shifted to the person: “you didn't take care of it yourself”.
- **Signal:** A doctor is personally responsible because of their qualifications; with AI it's unclear who is (a participant's experience; his wife is a doctor).
- **Heard at the game:** “You were warned, so check”
- **Step for this week:** Before an important decision based on AI advice, check it as if you were signing it with your own name.

## Autonomy world (DS)

Nobody has designed this world for this question yet.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Problems are solved at the level where they arise.**

- **How it develops:** The state is unnoticeable, like an automatic payment: people organise work, study and health themselves and turn to it in exceptional cases. A problem goes higher only if it can't be solved below. Institutions are temporary: task solved, they dissolve.
- **The price:** Who controls the controllers? Order may rest on a hidden force.
- **Signal:** Elinor Ostrom (Nobel Prize): shared resources can be managed by a community without control from above.
- **Heard at the game:** “If I can change the brake pads myself, nobody needs to inspect”
- **Step for this week:** Pick one problem you pass “upstairs”, and try to solve it yourself or with neighbours.
