## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Computation is taxed: every digital operation is visible and counted.**

- **How it develops:** Taxes have always been levied on what's visible: land and salt, then trade, then labour, then consumption. The next visible thing is computation and tokens. The budget is automated and collection transparent. The more your life takes up space in computation, the more you pay.
- **The price:** The most active leave, and underground data centres and “scam states” grow. Personal data and the personal computer become a frontier being fought over.
- **Signal:** An electronically issued invoice already counts as a VAT event, paid or not; the same in China (according to the host).
- **Heard at the game:** “Taxes are always levied on what's visible”
- **Step for this week:** Work out what share of your work already goes through digital services and which of it you could do by hand and with people.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**The mobilisation model: those tied to a place pay.**

- **How it develops:** The state keeps control of those who can't leave: factories, extraction, offline outlets. What's collected goes to AI development and state priorities. Digital sectors get breaks and subsidies; immobile business gets the burden.
- **The price:** An economy of extortion: laws are observed for show, and resources change hands by force. Investing in the real sector is risky; the rules can change at any moment.
- **Signal:** Big business moves its legal address to another region and pays taxes there rather than where it operates (a participant's example).
- **Heard at the game:** “Where you were born is where you're useful”
- **Step for this week:** Check how tied to a place your business is, and what you'd have left if the rules changed tomorrow.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Tax as a platform: rules are written by global corporations, with a minimum rate for everyone.**

- **How it develops:** Tax is levied on profit and capital through a shared grid: wherever you reduce a payment, it's collected elsewhere. The biggest players influence policy on a par with states. There's e-residency: a country's infrastructure without moving. Small businesses below the threshold pay almost nothing.
- **The price:** Your position is determined not by your relationship with the state but by your relationship with a corporation. If the world sways and the holder of power changes, nobody will pay for the weak.
- **Signal:** A global minimum tax of 15% for large corporations: if they paid 2% in one jurisdiction, the rest is collected in another (a game card).
- **Heard at the game:** “As long as the strongest agrees, everything holds”
- **Step for this week:** Find out which digital services of other countries are available to you without moving.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Vote with your tax: the base part is compulsory, and you direct the rest yourself.**

- **How it develops:** The minimum goes to security and social services, and a person allocates part of their tax to areas that matter to them. Communities gather around those areas. Local self-government decides how to spend, as in a homeowners' association or in countries where budgets are decided locally.
- **The price:** An influencer could pull the money towards “bananas in Siberia”, and everything else would fail. A closed list of areas and trust in the system are needed.
- **Signal:** A participant regularly sends money to authors he reads and buys game tickets not only for himself: “I support what I like”.
- **Heard at the game:** “I vote with my tax”
- **Step for this week:** This week, support one cause nearby that matters to you, with your money or your time.
