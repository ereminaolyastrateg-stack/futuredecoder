**How will people earn money if there isn't work for everyone?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Pay will be tied to the minimum, with a bonus for what matters to the state.**

- **How it develops:** Jobs are distributed by quotas; you need passes and certificates. Pay is the regional subsistence minimum plus a bonus for sectors important to the state. Those who guard and secure grow: security staff, the military.
- **The price:** People no longer create the new but guard what exists; the artist gets the minimum.
- **Signal:** Pay is falling everywhere, but security specialists are doing fine (observation of a participant working in cybersecurity).
- **Heard at the game:** “The human and a safe world”
- **Step for this week:** Look at which tasks in your profession are about “guarding and controlling” and which are about “creating”.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**There's no salary: you work as much as you've agreed.**

- **How it develops:** Access to resources (water, air) gets more expensive; everyone survives as they can. The main institution is science, and scientists become priests. Everyone else negotiates work themselves or takes part in experiments.
- **The price:** The polarisation of rich and poor reaches biology and access to water.
- **Signal:** Tutors suggest: take one lesson on the platform, and then we'll agree directly for less.
- **Heard at the game:** “You work as much as you've agreed”
- **Step for this week:** Think what you could agree on directly, without an intermediary or a platform.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Not a right to work but a right to capital.**

- **How it develops:** There isn't work for everyone, and earnings detach from labour. Capital comes in different forms: intellectual (a digital twin runs some of your consultations), property (a generation passes flats to only children), shares and assets. You labour to be useful and earn in another way.
- **The price:** It's scary: “stocks are evil” sits in your head, and investing is a separate job, not “buy and clip coupons”.
- **Signal:** For many young Africans trading is the only way to earn “for a washing machine” (from the host's project).
- **Heard at the game:** “Labour and finance are two parallel lines”
- **Step for this week:** Split it on paper: what you do to be useful, and where money could come from besides a salary.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Two worlds: corporations pay by grade, communities by ability.**

- **How it develops:** Corporations set the norms and fit people to business needs. Next to them live autonomous households with almost no pay: there are agreements and a minimum income. If the system threw someone out because of age or illness, the community will take them in, together with their experience.
- **The price:** The decentralised world may break into small clans where everyone looks out for their own.
- **Signal:** Small communities and settlements build themselves: they buy land and invent their own rules.
- **Heard at the game:** “Autonomous households will take in whoever the system threw out”
- **Step for this week:** Find a community nearby that could use your experience, and offer help once.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Everything is possible, so everything is impossible** (Все). If you can choose even the kind of trouble you get, you feel freer. Imagine a world where it's the norm, figure out how to live there, and it's no longer scary.

- **Paradox:** Living through a bad scenario in advance frees your brain for imagination.
- **Experiment for a week:** Pack a “go-bag” for one of your fears, and see what frees up in your head.
