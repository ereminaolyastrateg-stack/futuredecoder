## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**From outside: pills, purchases and services tune it.**

- **How it develops:** Happiness can be tuned from outside: a pill, a purchase, an experience, a sensor. A person here is a mechanism, and happiness is a sign the mechanism works well.
- **The price:** It's more “not being unhappy” than happiness, plus dependence on whoever does the tuning.
- **Signal:** The growth of psychopharmacology reinforces the idea that mood can be managed through biology.
- **Heard at the game:** “Happiness is not being unhappy”
- **Step for this week:** Look at what controls your mood from outside right now and decide what dose you keep.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**From indicators: it's measured and managed.**

- **How it develops:** Happiness is an indicator. States and companies measure it, make decisions by it and compare countries.
- **The price:** What gets measured gets managed: happiness becomes a tool of control.
- **Signal:** Bhutan measures Gross National Happiness with a large questionnaire.
- **Heard at the game:** “If we measure something, we can manage it”
- **Step for this week:** Find out who measures your happiness and how (work, an app, a survey) and what they do with the numbers.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**From self-study: you measure and tune yourself.**

- **How it develops:** Happiness can be studied: separated from yourself, measured through sleep, steps and pulse, broken down into its “elementary particles” and tuned by yourself, without outside crutches.
- **The price:** The myth that happiness can be taken out of a person, cleaned and put back. Like a car that gets washed more than it gets driven.
- **Signal:** A movement of constant self-tracking emerges: people regularly measure sleep, movement, pulse and productivity.
- **Heard at the game:** “Happiness is something you can pull out, clean, oil and put back”
- **Step for this week:** For a week, write down not numbers but moments when you felt good, and what your body was doing then.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**From processes with people: it's sustained together.**

- **How it develops:** Happiness is not a state but a process. It comes from outside in, through people, place and shared work, and you can't get it once and for all.
- **The price:** It has to be constantly sustained through relationships and participation.
- **Signal:** Eating together is linked to higher life satisfaction.
- **Heard at the game:** “You can't just flip it: start living well and then do nothing”
- **Step for this week:** Invite someone to eat together this week, for no reason.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Quiet happiness or visibility** (DS + CE). Happiness loves quiet, but to earn money you have to be visible.

- **Paradox:** I want to quietly tend my garden, and have everyone know me and pay a lot
- **Experiment for a week:** For a week, separate where you must be visible and where you can disappear.

**Focus on yourself or on connections** (DS + DE). Focusing on your body and state brings happiness, but weakens the ties through which everything else comes.

- **Paradox:** How do you stay part of the world while focusing on yourself?
- **Experiment for a week:** Do one thing for yourself and one for someone else on the same day, and compare how it feels.
