**How will shared decisions work if voting doesn't suit everyone?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**The centre decides, and everyone pays.**

- **How it develops:** The committee listened to everyone and made a decision; essentially it's the same single centre. A building voted 60 to 40 to replace the lifts, and ground-floor residents are obliged to take out a loan. If the centre errs, ordinary people suffer.
- **The price:** Voting gives the most obvious, linear decisions, and the conflict remains.
- **Signal:** In Spain a building voted to replace lifts at about €20,000 per flat; the minority must pay (an example from the host's acquaintances).
- **Heard at the game:** “They decide, we pay”
- **Step for this week:** Before a shared decision, ask: who will pay for it, and who will answer if it doesn't work?

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**You vote, but only from the list you were offered.**

- **How it develops:** The system widens the rules for the active and keeps the benefits for itself. Out of twenty options you vote for the top ten. Same in the family: to avoid arguing about “where shall we go”, you're given a list of five options.
- **The price:** Managed expansion: the bigger the field, the more pretexts for control. Freedom can't be obtained by consent; it can only be taken.
- **Signal:** Weighted voting “by merit” was discussed at the game; participants think it degenerates into “measuring skulls”.
- **Heard at the game:** “Out of twenty, you vote for the top ten”
- **Step for this week:** In your next family or team dispute, offer a short list rather than one option, and agree on who compiles it.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**The decision is made “by nobody”: it's easier to think it wasn't you.**

- **How it develops:** Choice is handed to an algorithm, the chat majority, a coin toss. “Crooked democracy”: we admit the consequences are ours but hope it will sort itself out or AI will decide. In the end someone's patience snaps: “I've bought it all, chip in.”
- **The price:** Responsibility is blurred, decisions are postponed, and the most impatient end up pulling the load.
- **Signal:** The homeowners' building chat as a model: “the building manager will sort it all out” (participants' example).
- **Heard at the game:** “Sometimes it's easier to think the decision wasn't yours”
- **Step for this week:** Notice one decision you put “to a vote” to avoid deciding yourself, and make it.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**People look for a win-win in a difficult dialogue, not a majority.**

- **How it develops:** The decision should be a weighted average so everyone is fine. Two opposing vectors, from above and from below, keep looking for balance, like management and unions. From below there's its own monitoring of the upper system. The best ideas often belong to the minority: they're not yet fully formulated.
- **The price:** It's slow and difficult; when time is short, people still vote.
- **Signal:** Goldratt: profit, care for employees and the client's growth are three equally important goals of a company (according to the host).
- **Heard at the game:** “This isn't democracy, it's a search for a negotiating position”
- **Step for this week:** In your next dispute, give the floor to whoever is in the minority and ask them to explain their idea fully.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Physically fragmented, intellectually connected** (Все). Borders close, factories relocate, yet the world's knowledge is available to everyone. Each person carries their own set of world pictures, which is why trends are so hard to predict.

- **Paradox:** We've never been so physically fragmented and so intellectually connected.
- **Experiment for a week:** Write down where you get information from, and see what kind of world it adds up to for you.

**A person can be a system too** (Все). A blogger or visionary is a face with infrastructure behind it. They sell an idea, and people only join, deciding nothing. The centre isn't necessarily a corporation, and the decentralised isn't necessarily humanity.

- **Paradox:** When the big cycle changes, everything flips like a mirror: what was at the bottom goes to the top.
- **Experiment for a week:** Check one blogger you follow: can you influence them in anything at all?
