**How will getting rid of bad habits work?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

Nobody has designed this world for this question yet.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Your surroundings decide more than willpower.**

- **How it develops:** If nobody around you drinks, quitting is easy; when everyone in the company is for it, it's hard. Responsibility lies in stopping in time and avoiding situations where you can't stop. A harmful pleasure can become a rare ritual instead of being banned.
- **The price:** You may not move to longevity even if you want to, if your environment pulls you back.
- **Signal:** Surroundings determine success more than individual effort
- **Heard at the game:** Life is determined by environment and surroundings.
- **Step for this week:** Look at the five people you spend most time with: which habits do you pick up from them?

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**A habit isn't banned but replaced with something that meets its cause.**

- **How it develops:** A bad habit always has a cause. James Clear suggests replacing a habit with a new one, and systems thinking (Goldratt) suggests finding the cause and closing it. Example: a robot vacuum instead of cleaning, and the freed time goes on a walk. A new habit can also tip out of balance; it needs watching.
- **The price:** What becomes harmful is what has gone out of balance. Taken to the absolute, the pluses become the norm and go unnoticed, while the minuses break the system.
- **Signal:** Replacing a habit instead of fighting it
- **Step for this week:** Take one habit and write down what need it meets and what else could meet that need.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**The pill and the anxiety** (ЦС + ЦЗ). There's a drug against ageing, yet health anxiety only grows.

- **Paradox:** More health data, more anxiety.
- **Experiment for a week:** Don't check your tracker metrics for a week, and see how you feel.

**The health paradox** (ЦЗ). The more you invest in health, the more you need to invest; health becomes a burden.

- **Paradox:** The goal of “more health” takes resources from the meaning it was wanted for.
- **Experiment for a week:** Lay out your desired future by time, money, attention and meaning, and check whether the day has enough hours.

**Limited or free time** (ЦС / ДС). A short life forces you to choose; a long one lets you try things, but removes the external motivator.

- **Paradox:** Without a ticking clock, the reason to stop disappears.
- **Experiment for a week:** Start one thing you put off “because it's too late”.
