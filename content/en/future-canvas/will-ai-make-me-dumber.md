**How will thinking work when answers come ready-made?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**A ready answer replaces the search, and there's no way back.**

- **How it develops:** A standard request gets a standard answer. Everyone chases “how to ask properly”; hardly anyone writes their own prompts. Everyone understands each other, but they all create the same thing. Once you're used to ready answers, you won't go back to deep reasoning.
- **The price:** The skill of setting a task and building an algorithm is lost. Thinking becomes “mathematical” rather than alive.
- **Signal:** Stories written with AI were rated as more creative, but different people's work became more alike.
- **Heard at the game:** “Each person is more creative, the culture more uniform”
- **Step for this week:** Solve one everyday problem yourself: write out the steps before asking AI.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**You can't compete with AI on brains, so people compete with bodies.**

- **How it develops:** When you can't stand out with your mind, people look for what AI doesn't have: a body. It's easier to pump up your body than your brain, especially with supplements and clubs. An unexpected conclusion: healthy people also think better; mental clarity comes from sport.
- **The price:** An endless race to be “above the neighbour”; satisfaction only in moments of victory. What if improving the body lowers cognition?
- **Signal:** China held the first robot championship: robots run faster than people.
- **Heard at the game:** “It's easier to pump up your body than your brain”
- **Step for this week:** Add one regular physical activity as part of your working day, not separate from it.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**When you write, you think; what becomes valuable is what you do with your own hands and head.**

- **How it develops:** Using AI is fashionable now, but later not using it will be fashionable: writing with a marker on a flipchart when everyone has identical slides. If AI writes for us, it's unclear where thinking moves to. Perhaps AI skills will one day be like vocational school, and the elite skill will be the ability to think and get a point across.
- **The price:** Stratification: some degrade with AI, some develop. What matters is being able to use AI for your own good.
- **Signal:** A signal: AI can slow down experts' work.
- **Heard at the game:** “When we write, we think”
- **Step for this week:** Write one thought by hand that you'd normally give to AI, and compare.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**AI teaches you to formulate rather than thinking for you.**

- **How it develops:** At school, books and sport go side by side. Debate clubs: AI helps you learn to formulate rather than handing out ready arguments. Lots of attempts; randomness is valuable: new directions are born from many crazy attempts.
- **The price:** It takes effort: you have to grow in two directions at once, your field and technology.
- **Signal:** The host: AI's basic function is the interviewer: “write a guide of 100 questions to figure out what I should do”.
- **Heard at the game:** “It doesn't produce the arguments for you, it helps”
- **Step for this week:** Ask AI to ask you questions about your idea instead of giving an answer.
