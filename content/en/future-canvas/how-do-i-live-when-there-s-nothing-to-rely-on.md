**How will people live when there are no more guarantees?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**The system promises safety and tightens control, and people get around it.**

- **How it develops:** Temporary measures after a crisis become permanent: passes, permits, ratings. The system always wants more control, and people always find a workaround: colleagues' masks at the turnstile, VPNs. I understand the system's logic, so little surprises me: “we'll figure something out”.
- **The price:** Measures are easy to introduce, but nobody takes responsibility for lifting them; the end date and conditions are never set.
- **Signal:** In China people got around face recognition at the turnstile by wearing masks of their colleagues.
- **Heard at the game:** “Design any system on the assumption that it will be bypassed”
- **Step for this week:** Recall one rule in your life or work that was once introduced “temporarily”, and decide whether you still need it.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**There are no guarantees: searching becomes the normal state.**

- **How it develops:** Don't wait for pensions and social security; there's no single right path: some move, some buy a jacket with ventilation, some inject themselves with an upgrade. I'm always searching and focused. When this is the norm, you don't whine; you look for opportunities.
- **The price:** It's all your responsibility: “inject the wrong thing and you've passed evolution on to the next one”.
- **Signal:** Workers ask to move shifts to another time of day because of the heat, and work clothing becomes a life-support system.
- **Heard at the game:** “When it's the norm, you don't whine; you look for opportunities”
- **Step for this week:** Pick one area where you're waiting for guarantees and take a small step there without them.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Don't belong to a single “anthill”: several jobs at once.**

- **How it develops:** I work in several directions at once, not one after another. I change industries, but my experience stays with me: I left law to do nails, and people still come to me for advice. When one line drops, I quickly move to the other.
- **The price:** You need the skill of holding several things at once, and platform ratings decide who finds you.
- **Signal:** People deliberately don't want to be “a single drone”: side work beyond the main job is growing.
- **Heard at the game:** “Experience never goes to waste”
- **Step for this week:** Write down two things besides your main work that you could do right now based on your experience.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**People try new rules of life themselves, and learn to negotiate.**

- **How it develops:** The old ways of living don't work, from earning to family. People create temporary societies to test how to live, and live in several digital circuits at once. Everyone's reality is different, so the key skill is reaching agreement.
- **The price:** Agreeing is hard: everyone is sure their reality is the only one, and the risk of conflict grows.
- **Signal:** People create a temporary society of about 500 people to test how to live.
- **Heard at the game:** “Reality is a matter of agreement”
- **Step for this week:** Talk to someone who sees the situation differently and find one point in common.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**A temporary measure with no end date** (Все). Measures are introduced after a crisis, and nobody takes responsibility for lifting them, so unnecessary rules pile up. It's the same with children: when do you decide they can go out alone?

- **Paradox:** People take responsibility for introducing a rule; nobody takes it for lifting one.
- **Experiment for a week:** Find one of your “temporary” rules and set a review date for it.

**The system is us** (Все). Dystopias don't describe the elite. The people at the top don't know what to do either, and they ask people what they want. A closed loop with no beginning or end.

- **Paradox:** We look for whoever controls us, while we control ourselves.
- **Experiment for a week:** Ask yourself “who does this depend on?” instead of “who's to blame?”.
