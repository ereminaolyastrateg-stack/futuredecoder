**How will people's relationship with money work: an end or a means?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

Nobody has designed this world for this question yet.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Those with little money think about money; those with a lot measure by other things.**

- **How it develops:** Money has become an end in itself, though Adam Smith wrote about the wealth of nations. Polarisation: those with little focus on money, those with a lot measure by other categories. Money is also a media and symbolic phenomenon.
- **The price:** The stronger the focus on money, the less you see of everything else.
- **Signal:** Money has become an end in itself
- **Step for this week:** Write down what, besides money, you use to measure whether life is going well.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Small playful steps take away the fear of money.**

- **How it develops:** The host: fear of money goes away not through study but through small steps you can try without big losses. Discipline is putting what you already have in order, not learning something new.
- **The price:** The complexity barrier: it feels like you need to understand everything first.
- **Signal:** Discipline = order in what you have
- **Step for this week:** Put one account or one spending category in order; don't learn anything new.

## Community world (DE)

Nobody has designed this world for this question yet.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Finances in four quadrants** (Все). Part on autopilot, part by the rules, part owned, part in a shared cause. Plus a stress scenario at the minimum.

- **Paradox:** Leaning into one quadrant is convenient until a crisis hits.
- **Experiment for a week:** Lay out your money across the four quadrants and see which is empty.

**Money: a means or a product** (ЦС / ДЗ). Finance has become a product, though it should be a service. Rational decisions produce an irrational result.

- **Paradox:** More money, less wellbeing (according to the host).
- **Experiment for a week:** Over a week, mark which expenses served your life and which served someone's product.
