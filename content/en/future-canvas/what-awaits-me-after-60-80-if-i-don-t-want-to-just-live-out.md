**How will old age be organised?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Ageing is a business, while the pension system is cracking.**

- **How it develops:** Rings, bracelets and sensors measure everything measurable; ageing is turned into a business. There aren't enough pensions for everyone: either save yourself, work longer, or children support their parents. You can fit in and turn your habit into a product.
- **The price:** You're needed by the system while you're fit for use; there are no new people, “nobody left to milk”.
- **Signal:** A pensioner fed pigeons and turned it into a walk for tourists on an experiences platform (invented at the game).
- **Heard at the game:** “While the system is developing, make the most of its benefits on the way in”
- **Step for this week:** Take one of your habits or hobbies and think about who might find it interesting as a service.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Slowing ageing is your choice and your responsibility.**

- **How it develops:** The infrastructure of gadgets and sensors is provided; after that, you manage on your own. Want to work at 70? Work, just not slaving away but doing deep and varied things. You can invent a profession associated only with mature age.
- **The price:** The limit is you: whoever lacks strength and money is left without all of this.
- **Signal:** The profession of “village CEO”, but with an age limit of 40, which raised a question at the game: how old should a manager be?
- **Heard at the game:** “We're those 4% who made it this far; it's not scary”
- **Step for this week:** Think which work you only get better at with age, and find one person over 70 who does it.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**First you work, then you teach: the experience of elders is valued again.**

- **How it develops:** There's no idea of retiring: “I'll work, and then I'll teach.” Old age is being re-evaluated: old isn't whoever the system doesn't need. But there's no infrastructure for passing on experience yet; you have to build it yourself.
- **The price:** Elders want to give, and the young don't yet want to receive.
- **Signal:** The creative council of one cosmetics corporation includes people of every five-year age group from 10 to 80 (according to the host).
- **Heard at the game:** “We have the desire to give; the young don't yet have the desire to receive”
- **Step for this week:** Find one person about 30 years younger and offer to work through their problem together.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Health is held up by the environment, and generations talk as equals.**

- **How it develops:** Health isn't particularly monitored; people just don't wreck it, and the environment supports it. Old and young live and do things together, without the “grandchildren and grandma” hierarchy. Interest clubs appear that people of any age attend.
- **The price:** This may turn out to be wishful thinking: why would the young want it, what's their motivation?
- **Signal:** Long-lived Nobel laureates of the early 20th century all taught and spent time with young people (from the host's research).
- **Heard at the game:** “Students and elders aren't grandchildren and grandparents; there's no hierarchy”
- **Step for this week:** Go to a club or group with people of different ages, and talk to the oldest and the youngest.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**A big family under one roof** (CS + DE). Corporations hand part of the activity to people, and expensive square metres force people to move in together: multigenerational homes, the “Boston marriage”.

- **Paradox:** You have to reach maximum fragmentation to start uniting.
- **Experiment for a week:** Invite your parents or children to dinner together with the question “what are you living for right now?”.

**Forecast or project** (Все). A forecast guesses the realistic and fits into it; a project sees all the options and chooses what to take for itself.

- **Paradox:** Statistics are always about the past: you decide whether to be in the 96% or the 4%.
- **Experiment for a week:** Take one forecast about yourself (age, profession) and write down which option you'd choose if there were no forecast.

**Doing good is hard** (Все). A “students for active elders” service will run into precedents from TV series where the young cheat the old.

- **Paradox:** Media is the easiest lever, and also the easiest way to do harm.
- **Experiment for a week:** Find one story where generations helped each other, and tell it.
