**How will social guarantees work when there are fewer workers than people to support?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**What was free becomes a paid market.**

- **How it develops:** Social taxes were sold as insurance: you chip in now and get support later. The pyramid has flipped: for every worker there are five pensioners and five sick people. AI partly takes over medicine and education, and budget provision shrinks. The host bets that people will pay for quality themselves.
- **The price:** You pay, and the insurance doesn't work. Public-sector workers are moved to the minimum, cut and merged.
- **Signal:** Parents in the regions complain their children aren't admitted to year 10 even with good exam results (news the host saw).
- **Heard at the game:** “We can't abandon them, and we can no longer help them”
- **Step for this week:** Check which state guarantees you're counting on, and what you'll do if they aren't there.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Public-sector workers split into the needed and the rest.**

- **How it develops:** Some work at guarded state plants and with state AI: the peak of a career, with queues to get in. Others perform functions and get crumbs. Public-sector workers pay almost no taxes; the burden falls on producers, especially small ones.
- **The price:** Stability exists only for those who landed in the right place. The rest get a minimum with no guarantees.
- **Signal:** Doctors, even in state clinics, build a personal brand and take side jobs (a participant's observation).
- **Heard at the game:** “Maybe I'll go and work for the state”
- **Step for this week:** Think which of these groups you're closer to and what you're doing so as not to depend on one.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Shared functions are paid for jointly.**

- **How it develops:** Corporations don't need public-sector workers, but the functions remain: rubbish collection, culture, help. They're paid by subscription and locally: “want your rubbish collected? Chip in.” The public-sector worker lives like a monk, on what was collected.
- **The price:** Whoever didn't chip in goes without the service. When the holder of power changes, the weak are abandoned first.
- **Signal:** In Brazil residents began deciding for the first time where taxes go (schools, roads, water) in response to distrust of officials (a game card).
- **Heard at the game:** “Want the theatre? Chip in”
- **Step for this week:** Look at which shared services in your building or neighbourhood already run on voluntary contributions.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Support remains for those with nowhere else to go, and culture as a common good.**

- **How it develops:** Free is no longer the universal norm: whoever can goes private. The public sector remains for the low-income. Besides it, participants kept public amenities and culture: the host sees culture as a supplier of positive images of the future and a platform for dialogue. Decisions rest on trust: if people believe the system is fair, they take part.
- **The price:** Everyone pays for the minority, and this has to be proved constantly. Those who think the system is unfair leave.
- **Signal:** A participant deliberately goes into the system: she installed a cash register and is glad of a single medical record holding all her tests.
- **Heard at the game:** “Against a world where there's no money for insurance, and die wherever you like”
- **Step for this week:** Find one cultural place nearby that matters to you, and go there or support it.
