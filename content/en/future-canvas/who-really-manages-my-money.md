**How will personal finances work in ten years, and who will manage them?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Finance as a subscription to life: the pot is cooking somewhere, and you just use it.**

- **How it develops:** Everything moves to subscriptions and rentals: “you don't own, you only have access”. A fund manages a person's wellbeing and draws up a five-year roadmap. Business moves everything to subscriptions “so the person always stays with you”.
- **The price:** The person is a resource with zero agency. Dependence grows exactly where we delegate decisions. Household debt grows.
- **Signal:** Property by subscription: no ownership, only access
- **Heard at the game:** A subscription to life, renting, autopilot.
- **Step for this week:** List all your subscriptions and mark which decisions autopilot already makes for you.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Finance as a rating of the right life: money is the measure of meeting expectations.**

- **How it develops:** Wellness banking: the bank looks at biometrics and lifestyle. Stablecoins are legalised, but the state sets the rules. The routine is meeting expectations and staying on the wave.
- **The price:** You take part yourself, but the result doesn't belong to you. People dislike the rules. But whoever has learned discipline and counting will be “hard to fool”.
- **Signal:** Wellness banking: an ATM measures blood pressure, the bank takes biometrics into account
- **Heard at the game:** Finance as a measure of expectations.
- **Step for this week:** Work out what share of your income goes on mandatory spending, and compare it with last year.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Money works for you: keep it, preserve it, grow it.**

- **How it develops:** Self-custody infrastructure and P2P lending are growing. Banks have lending KPIs; P2P doesn't, so taking part is more voluntary. The principle of “not earning but not spending”, health as an asset.
- **The price:** The complexity threshold: to own things yourself, you have to understand them. Fear of money.
- **Signal:** Self-custody infrastructure for assets
- **Heard at the game:** Making money work.
- **Step for this week:** Ask yourself: what exactly am I afraid of in finance, what of it is irreversible, and how likely is it?

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Finance as leverage: you create opportunities and set the rules.**

- **How it develops:** Human-centred capital management. Involving the next generation: family offices bring young people into decisions. Shared resources with shared rules (Elinor Ostrom). Some people live on donations, which is a high level of trust.
- **The price:** Maximum agency means maximum responsibility. You have to choose: react or create.
- **Signal:** Family offices: 76% involve the younger generation in decisions (game card)
- **Heard at the game:** Finance as self-realisation.
- **Step for this week:** Find one shared cause you're ready to invest in together with others on clear rules.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Finances in four quadrants** (Все). Part on autopilot, part by the rules, part owned, part in a shared cause. Plus a stress scenario at the minimum.

- **Paradox:** Leaning into one quadrant is convenient until a crisis hits.
- **Experiment for a week:** Lay out your money across the four quadrants and see which is empty.

**Money: a means or a product** (CS / DE). Finance has become a product, though it should be a service. Rational decisions produce an irrational result.

- **Paradox:** More money, less wellbeing (according to the host).
- **Experiment for a week:** Over a week, mark which expenses served your life and which served someone's product.
