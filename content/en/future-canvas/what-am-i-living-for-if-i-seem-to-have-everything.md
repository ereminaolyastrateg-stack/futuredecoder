**How will the search for meaning in life work?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**The system issues meaning, and you can find your own within it.**

- **How it develops:** Every system answers why you live: religion, the state, the corporation (“bake the tastiest doughnuts”). The state more often gives comfort and control than meaning. You can find your own meanings inside the system's meanings (family, a vegetable garden, service) or use it as a source of money and live your own life in your free time.
- **The price:** The system can exclude you without explanation. If money has stopped mattering to you inside a corporation, it's hard there.
- **Signal:** A government service offers document packages in advance: “you haven't bought a house yet, but we've already prepared everything” (a participant's example).
- **Heard at the game:** “Find your own meanings within the system's meanings”
- **Step for this week:** Describe what you'll do if the system that feeds you disappears, and what you'll have left then.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Meaning lies in belonging: joining a movement.**

- **How it develops:** You unite situationally around a big goal or justice, around a visionary or a blogger. A small project gives a tangible result, and you feel you have influence. You can try different movements and switch if it's not yours.
- **The price:** Meaning is being sold: sects, info-gurus, “gurus”. All that glitters is not gold, but total rejection also makes you more vulnerable.
- **Signal:** Barcelona: chess clubs for trans people, a rave in a sauna; small communities made of two unexpected things (the host's observation).
- **Heard at the game:** “Belonging is also part of meaning”
- **Step for this week:** Join one situational effort where the result will be visible within a month.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**The search for meaning is handed to AI and replaced with configuring agents.**

- **How it develops:** “Study my messages and profile, find ten possible meanings.” Agents do everything, the person makes no effort, yet truth is born in the search itself. An algorithm can help find similar “lost” people to search together.
- **The price:** Irrelevant answers, small print, more contact with technology than with people. Time goes into the agents: an “illusion of autonomy”.
- **Signal:** A participant limited her time online, started reading more and learning to tolerate boredom, and became calmer and slept better.
- **Heard at the game:** “Instead of searching for meaning, we configure agents that are supposed to find it”
- **Step for this week:** Before asking AI, write down your three values and check its answers against them.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Meaning is found together with those who share a similar image of the future.**

- **How it develops:** People look for their own kind and decide whom to connect with. You belong to several communities at once. The first step is doing something for others for free: improving a courtyard, coaching children, helping older people with the internet.
- **The price:** Hard choices and lots of effort; it's easy to overdo the contrast: “I was at rock bottom, now I'm mindful” doesn't work like that.
- **Signal:** An IT worker started improving a dreary high-rise courtyard for children in his spare time and filming it (a participant's example).
- **Heard at the game:** “Start doing something for free and giving a piece of yourself”
- **Step for this week:** Do one thing for free for the people around you this week.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Minimal comfort and a great goal** (CS + CE). For the last 60 years marketing has sold a selfish image of the future: “do it for yourself”. But people's need to do something for others and be part of something shared remains unmet. The host believes minimal comfort with a great goal beats maximum comfort without one.

- **Paradox:** Comfort kills movement, and a goal demands giving up part of your comfort.
- **Experiment for a week:** Find one thing where your contribution is needed not by you but by others.
