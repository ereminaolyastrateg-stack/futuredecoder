**What will people pay for comfort?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Comfort is the system's bait: you pay more for it than you get.**

- **How it develops:** You can't get into someone's head against their will, so people are lured with convenience: “we'll do everything for you”. Leave one big platform and you're immediately invited to another, with ready infrastructure. Comfortable now, and in three to five years the same dependence.
- **The price:** The long-term price of comfort is higher than the comfort itself: “like Baba Yaga, who feeds you before putting you in the oven”.
- **Signal:** Entrepreneurs whose goods burned in a marketplace warehouse are moving en masse to a big bank's platform.
- **Heard at the game:** “Evolution contradicts comfort”
- **Step for this week:** Find one dependence on a convenient service and take the first step towards your own option, for example your own website instead of a platform.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Freedom has a price: stay in the comfortable system or break free.**

- **How it develops:** You can live in the system with all its benefits and take your tests, or leave for the countryside, provide for yourself and answer to no one. The rich already buy themselves a little real hardship: they grow coffee on their own land. It's not a step towards comfort but deliberate discomfort.
- **The price:** No guarantees: the further from the beaten track, the less insurance.
- **Signal:** The rich buy themselves a little real hardship: they grow coffee and mangoes on their own land.
- **Heard at the game:** “Either steak in the matrix or porridge in freedom”
- **Step for this week:** Pick one growth-inducing hardship for the week: a difficult text instead of short videos, cooking yourself instead of delivery.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**The employer takes on the care, and keeps you for years.**

- **How it develops:** The company provides housing, a subsidised mortgage and everything you need nearby; I live attached to it, like in an anthill. Business becomes a caring system and the state a faceless platform. Loyalty is rising again: people work in one place for decades.
- **The price:** As long as you're needed, you're cared for; fall ill or grow old and you may drop out.
- **Signal:** Employees of an IT company buy flats in Moscow with a subsidised corporate mortgage (the host's observation).
- **Heard at the game:** “Business becomes a caring system”
- **Step for this week:** Work out how much of your life depends on one employer, and what happens if it disappears.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**AI and robots take away routine, not for idleness but so you can labour with more quality.**

- **How it develops:** An AI agent restocks the shopping basket and manages finances: “my robot goes to work” while I'm on the treadmill. The freed time isn't for cross-stitching but for starting to strain again, with more quality.
- **The price:** It's easy to take prompts for your own thoughts and hand decisions to AI: a “spare cuckoo” instead of your own.
- **Signal:** AI agents already act on a person's behalf: they restock baskets and manage finances.
- **Heard at the game:** “To labour, not just to work”
- **Step for this week:** Give one routine task to AI and write down what you spent the freed hour on.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Comfort versus resilience** (CS + DS). A convenient platform now is dependence in three years; your own site and your own tool are pricier and harder, but they're about resilience.

- **Paradox:** Comfort in the moment and long-term resilience pull in different directions.
- **Experiment for a week:** Take one step towards your own tool instead of someone else's platform.
