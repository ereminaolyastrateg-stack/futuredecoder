## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Take a pill and stop thinking: health is bought, not built.**

- **How it develops:** A new class of anti-ageing drugs (senolytics) is reaching the market fast, cheaper and more affordable for the masses. AI sifts through compounds and finds new ones. The logic: “take a pill and you can booze”, save up for the drug.
- **The price:** Motivation to live healthily drops. Health is not quality of life: you can live long and be unhappy. A drug race may, as with antibiotics, breed resistance: “we cure one thing and cripple another”. The anxiety doesn't go away.
- **Signal:** A new class of anti-ageing drugs
- **Heard at the game:** Take a pill and you can booze.
- **Step for this week:** Ask yourself: if an anti-ageing pill already existed, what would you do with the extra years?

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Health is simpler than it seems: not a magic pill but the basics: sleep, food, movement, nature.**

- **How it develops:** A signal: basic practices (sleep, meditation, a plant-based diet) roll back epigenetic age by 2–3 years. Wearable trackers give more data, but more data means more anxiety. Exploratory behaviour and contact with nature heal both head and body. Pull up one area and you pull up the others.
- **The price:** It takes away harmful pleasures: “live fast, die young” won't work any more. It obliges you to invent new pleasures. It takes attention and discipline.
- **Signal:** An epigenetic rollback of 2–3 years from sleep, meditation and a plant-based diet
- **Heard at the game:** Don't overcomplicate it; start with the basics.
- **Step for this week:** Define your “service every 10,000 km”: how often you check your body, and put it in your calendar.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**The main upgrade is to stop making things worse for yourself.**

- **How it develops:** The focus is not on what to add but on what to remove. Signals: 80% of ageing is determined by epigenetics and actions, not genes. A habit isn't broken but replaced: first a new activity appears, then the old habit goes by itself. The key is to find the cause of the habit and meet it in a new way.
- **The price:** It takes more awareness and energy: living not on autopilot but asking yourself each time. Compensation: people quit drinking and switch to exhausting sport.
- **Signal:** The main upgrade is to stop making things worse for yourself
- **Heard at the game:** Stop making things worse for yourself.
- **Step for this week:** Before your next decision, ask: does this improve my future state or worsen it?

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Not active but happy longevity: with meaning, with a clear conscience, and with people.**

- **How it develops:** A signal: people with a sense of purpose have lower expression of inflammatory genes. According to the host, in her research the long-livers turned out to be scientists, musicians and Nobel laureates; what they share is work they love, contact across generations, simple food and going to bed early. Living by your conscience saves energy: remorse takes a great deal.
- **The price:** The word “active” suits employers: they expect your energy for themselves. “Happy” is broader: you can contemplate or write memoirs.
- **Signal:** A sense of purpose is linked to lower expression of inflammatory genes
- **Heard at the game:** Longevity can be happy in both active and passive forms.
- **Step for this week:** Find one goal that replenishes you as you move towards it, rather than draining you.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**The pill and the anxiety** (CS + CE). There's a drug against ageing, yet health anxiety only grows.

- **Paradox:** More health data, more anxiety.
- **Experiment for a week:** Don't check your tracker metrics for a week, and see how you feel.

**The health paradox** (CE). The more you invest in health, the more you need to invest; health becomes a burden.

- **Paradox:** The goal of “more health” takes resources from the meaning it was wanted for.
- **Experiment for a week:** Lay out your desired future by time, money, attention and meaning, and check whether the day has enough hours.

**Limited or free time** (CS / DS). A short life forces you to choose; a long one lets you try things, but removes the external motivator.

- **Paradox:** Without a ticking clock, the reason to stop disappears.
- **Experiment for a week:** Start one thing you put off “because it's too late”.
