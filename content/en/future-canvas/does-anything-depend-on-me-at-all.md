**Who will set the rules of life: the system or people themselves?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**The system sets the rules, and almost nothing is required of you.**

- **How it develops:** Money is programmable, data about you is collected by default, and the system can exclude you without explanation. The world is complex, and centralisation looks like a way to cope: “we centralised to make it easier, and then, oh, it's convenient”. There's no expansion, only control.
- **The price:** You don't make the decisions, but the consequences are still yours. If the system errs, ordinary people pay, not those who decided.
- **Signal:** Programmable state money: you can spend it in a shop but can't give it to a beggar (a participant's example).
- **Heard at the game:** “You don't bear responsibility, you bear consequences”
- **Step for this week:** Think ahead about what you'll do if a system you depend on excludes you tomorrow.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**There's a choice, but the menu wasn't written by you.**

- **How it develops:** The system needs you to take part yourself: the state messenger wasn't made so you'd log into public services, but so you'd chat there. It can't force you, so it makes things convenient and highlights its option. Other options exist, but they're “outside the lamplight”.
- **The price:** Comfort slowly declines, while attention is diverted to pleasant things: space, immortality. There is a choice, but it's expensive: quit your job, leave, lose your capital.
- **Signal:** There isn't a single public service you can't get offline; it's about convenience (according to the host).
- **Heard at the game:** “Choose your socks: brown, green or red”
- **Step for this week:** Find one service you use because “everyone does”, and check whether it has an alternative.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**The rules seem to be ours, but we hand decisions to agents, networks and influencers.**

- **How it develops:** I don't trust the system; I trust bloggers, communities and my AI agent. Early-warning networks watch the authorities themselves. A small, convenient system is built next to the big one. But a “black box” makes the decision for me, and I've just subscribed.
- **The price:** An illusion of self-control and a “tired rebellion”: I don't trust, but I sit on my phone watching other people's lives. “I'm not controlled, and I don't control.”
- **Signal:** Communities build their own early-warning networks and track the actions of security forces in real time.
- **Heard at the game:** “Whoever gets flooded calls the emergency service”
- **Step for this week:** In one shared matter (your building, a chat, a team), take one decision on yourself instead of waiting for someone's patience to snap.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Communities create the rules and regulate themselves.**

- **How it develops:** People unite by interest and by an image of the future; nobody forces them. Their own internet, their own legal personality, the state as a set of functions tailored to them. The consequences of your decisions show faster, so you feel that things depend on you.
- **The price:** It takes the ability to agree and live feedback; otherwise the community drifts to an extreme or upwards.
- **Signal:** In Nepal a new prime minister was chosen via Discord (according to the host).
- **Heard at the game:** “We have influence”
- **Step for this week:** Find one community where you can influence the rules, and propose one change.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**Physically fragmented, intellectually connected** (Все). Borders close, factories relocate, yet the world's knowledge is available to everyone. Each person carries their own set of world pictures, which is why trends are so hard to predict.

- **Paradox:** We've never been so physically fragmented and so intellectually connected.
- **Experiment for a week:** Write down where you get information from, and see what kind of world it adds up to for you.

**A person can be a system too** (Все). A blogger or visionary is a face with infrastructure behind it. They sell an idea, and people only join, deciding nothing. The centre isn't necessarily a corporation, and the decentralised isn't necessarily humanity.

- **Paradox:** When the big cycle changes, everything flips like a mirror: what was at the bottom goes to the top.
- **Experiment for a week:** Check one blogger you follow: can you influence them in anything at all?
