**How will my children learn?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**Along tracks the system picks early.**

- **How it develops:** The child was assessed on skills early, and the system suggested a track itself. Convenient and clear. But the earlier children are sorted into tracks, the wider the gap later. So we teach one thing: don't fear tests and don't fully trust them.
- **The price:** The child lands early in a track that is hard to leave.
- **Signal:** A study shows choosing an education path at 12 working as a mechanism of class division.
- **Heard at the game:** “The earlier we push children into an automated track, the bigger the gap between them”
- **Step for this week:** Look at which decisions about your child tests and grades already make, and discuss at least one with them.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**At a school of shared norms, under a mentor's eye.**

- **How it develops:** Children go to school for shared norms: reading, writing, negotiating, living by the rules. A mentor sees where to steer the child, and the child feels safe.
- **The price:** They're told what to learn, and there may be no great goal worth the effort.
- **Signal:** Prussia introduces compulsory mass schooling: reading, writing, standard textbooks and attendance control.
- **Heard at the game:** “Education as supervision, so they don't roam the streets”
- **Step for this week:** Find a mentor or a club that will guide your child for several years, not one season.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Discipline first, freedom later.**

- **How it develops:** We started with discipline: copybooks, counting and reading every day, even when it's boring. Copybooks teach the main thing: focus on the form of what you create. Freedom of choice came later.
- **The price:** It can hurt, and it's very important not to break the child.
- **Signal:** Productive failure: first the child tries and makes mistakes, and gets the explanation afterwards.
- **Heard at the game:** “Copybooks teach you to focus on the form of what you create”
- **Step for this week:** Pick one boring basic task for 15 minutes a day and do it together. Praise the sessions, not the top marks.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**From the world around them and from each other.**

- **How it develops:** My child learns from the world around: in neighbourhood labs, from craftspeople, together with other children. The teacher is no longer one per class; children teach each other.
- **The price:** Nobody guarantees the result; a lot depends on the adults around.
- **Signal:** Pluriversity at Dutch Design Week: the university as a network of real labs.
- **Heard at the game:** “The teacher leaves this loop, and people simply teach each other”
- **Step for this week:** Find an adult who can make things with their hands and ask them to take your child on as a helper for a couple of hours.
