**How will people look for work?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**The system picks you, if it needs your skills.**

- **How it develops:** Your purpose is issued from a menu, like a level in a game. The system needs people for pick-up points and delivery; my wishes aren't considered. But you can grow inside: careers are made by those who understand the whole process, not just their own task.
- **The price:** The right to work for money disappears: you work as long as the system needs you.
- **Signal:** Banks give AI tokens instead of cashback; money becomes an intermediary inside the system.
- **Heard at the game:** “Your purpose is issued from a menu”
- **Step for this week:** Take the job that's available now and learn the whole process around it, not just your piece.

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**Want it fast? Do it yourself: try things and show your solutions.**

- **How it develops:** A diploma goes out of date before I get it. So I try several directions in parallel and approach companies myself: I work through their problem and give them a solution for free. I'm ready for only a handful to respond, and I expect results in six months, not a week.
- **The price:** It takes time and effort: roughly one in fifty responds, and nothing happens within a week.
- **Signal:** A girl covered cheap shoes in feathers, and the video went viral.
- **Heard at the game:** “I solved your problem from my own angle; here it is, for free”
- **Step for this week:** Pick a company you like, solve their problem from your own angle and send it to them, for free.

## Autonomy world (DS)

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**Work is found through people: the environment will show you the path.**

- **How it develops:** Vacancies are filled before they're posted, through acquaintances and recommendations. I hang out with different people, but with the intention “I'm looking”, and I try whole environments: stay with someone, work where there are lots of people. The more visible I am, the more offers I get.
- **The price:** Trial and error: the environment also tells you where you don't belong, and being visible can hurt.
- **Signal:** Laid-off IT workers found jobs through acquaintances, not job sites: the strength of weak ties.
- **Heard at the game:** “Maybe you don't need job boards?”
- **Step for this week:** Tell five acquaintances, including your parents' friends, that you're looking, and ask them to introduce you to one person from a field you find interesting.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**A community of five** (CE + DE). Five friends each try their own direction and share results: five times more experience in the same time.

- **Paradox:** Everyone's skills differ; other people's experience doesn't always transfer.
- **Experiment for a week:** Gather two or three friends, agree that each tries one direction for a week, and call at the end.
