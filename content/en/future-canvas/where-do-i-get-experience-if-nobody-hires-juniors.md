**How will entry into a profession work if AI does the newcomer's work?** Four answers from four worlds of the Future Decoder method. They come from participants of the scenario games, not from experts.

## Autopilot world (CS)

*the system decides, the result is not yours*

**An algorithm chooses your path, based on school grades.**

- **How it develops:** University places are allocated by algorithms and needs. Good maths grades in year seven, and you're an engineer. Wanted to be a designer? Closed. I do art for myself, with books from my artist neighbour.
- **The price:** Everything is decided, cognitive load is almost zero, but there's almost no choice either.
- **Signal:** Chinese universities are betting on “one person, one business” (according to the host).
- **Heard at the game:** “The algorithm assigned me to maths”
- **Step for this week:** Ask yourself: what decision about you was made “by default”, and do you agree with it now?

## Rules world (CE)

*you take part by shared rules*

**What's needed is not a staff but one strong person; the rest maintain the infrastructure.**

- **How it develops:** A manager takes one person instead of thirty-five. “Everyone is the maker of their own happiness and a trendsetter.” Public-sector workers are those who maintain data centres and power grids, and few of them are needed.
- **The price:** The entry threshold keeps rising; there's nowhere to gain experience.
- **Signal:** Companies look for juniors less and less, seniors more.
- **Heard at the game:** “A kit of skills, but where do you get experience?”
- **Step for this week:** Find one task you could do “for a whole team” with AI, and make a trial version.

## Autonomy world (DS)

*you decide and you own it*

**Professions change, but your core competence stays.**

- **How it develops:** You may have a dozen professions in a lifetime (accountant, strategist, game organiser), yet everywhere you do the same thing. That's the core plus three adjacent skills. Once you understand what you're given, changing jobs isn't scary. Speaking well is a tool, not the core.
- **The price:** People often don't understand who they are: a doer pushes to become a manager and suffers.
- **Signal:** 60% of managers who grew up inside a big financial company don't like managing people; there was no other path to grow (the host's research).
- **Heard at the game:** “I don't mind where I bring order”
- **Step for this week:** List all your jobs and find one action you did in every one of them.

## Community world (DE)

*you create together with others*

**First find your strengths; then AI and a mentor will drill you.**

- **How it develops:** You can't be smart at everything, so first you try things and look for your strengths. AI can drill you up to specialist level, and a mentor who takes on responsibility helps where you're weak. You keep a “library of knowledge about yourself”: what came easily, what made you sick.
- **The price:** You can't sabotage your weak spots: three strengths and five minuses won't give balance.
- **Signal:** At a training, a participant found two mentors who build their own AI assistants.
- **Heard at the game:** “Drill yourself on your strengths”
- **Step for this week:** Start a list: what came easily to you, and what took effort.

## Mixes and paradoxes from this game

**The silent majority decides** (CS + DE). The majority think they decide nothing, yet every product is made to be “swallowed” by the majority. If it wanted to understand AI, everyone would be building mass education. Right now it's tired and wants “sort it out for us”.

- **Paradox:** Silence is also a choice, and it becomes the world's norm.
- **Experiment for a week:** Say out loud what you want from technology, not only what you fear.
