*The [[en/four-worlds/life-spheres/health|Health]] sphere in the [[en/four-worlds/centralized-synthetic|Centralized — Synthetic]] world: order through system, standard and reproducibility. Below is how this logic looks in each subsphere: a life situation, what it gives, what it costs and where it already exists.*

## Body and physical abilities

**Body support with service**

> If I need to improve or heal myself, I trust one clinic: it picks the treatment, monitors it and reminds me when to come for a check-up.

**What it gives.** Support for a specific function without having to master the whole technique; a clear contact for servicing.

**What it costs.** Dependence on the quality of fitting, consumables and service; help from a device doesn't develop your own abilities.

*Spends: Time, Money · Gives: Energy and health, Freedom of choice.*

**Where this already exists:**

- Known practice: I go to a workshop where they fit my glasses and later repair them. [source](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/assistive-technology)

## Habits, sleep and recovery

**Recovery by a ready-made programme**

> I use a ready-made sleep and nutrition programme with reminders.

**What it gives.** Less daily planning; easier to repeat the chosen routine.

**What it costs.** A ready routine may not match your life; without the programme the habit may not hold.

*Spends: Money, Freedom of choice · Gives: Time, Energy and health.*

**Where this already exists:**

- Known practice: I sleep and eat according to a sanatorium's fixed schedule. [source](https://veteranshealthlibrary.va.gov/LivingWith/Insomnia/Treatment/142%2C41434_VA)
- Today: I take a ready-made sleep recovery course based on a protocol. [source](https://mobile.va.gov/app/insomnia-coach)

## Mental health

**An accessible support service**

> I contact a service that matches me with a psychologist and arranges the session.

**What it gives.** Easier to make the first step; fewer organisational tasks in a hard period.

**What it costs.** Limited choice of specialists and formats; switching services can break continuity.

*Spends: Time, Money · Gives: Time, Energy and health.*

**Where this already exists:**

- Today: I get a consultation through an accessible support system. [source](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-06-2026-who-step-by-step-programme-recognized-as-a-promising-innovation-for-mental-health-in-humanitarian-settings)
- Experiment: I go through a structured remote help programme via a service. [source](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/B09738)

## Cognitive health

**Offloading memory to a ready assistant**

> My calendar reminds me about medicines, bills and important meetings.

**What it gives.** Fewer missed tasks and less load on memory; easy to start.

**What it costs.** Dependence on the service being available; too many prompts can distract and hide changes in your state.

*Spends: Money, Freedom of choice · Gives: Time, Energy and health.*

**Where this already exists:**

- Today: I hand reminders and notes to a ready-made digital service. [source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42350222/)

## Prevention, treatment, rehabilitation and care

**A route through a clinic or insurer**

> At the clinic a coordinator arranges my tests and collects the results.

**What it gives.** Less organisational load; it's clear who is responsible for the next step.

**What it costs.** Network and coverage limits; a standard route may handle an unusual case poorly.

*Spends: Money, Freedom of choice · Gives: Time, Energy and health.*

**Where this already exists:**

- Today: I book appointments and get prescriptions through a health service. [source](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/nhs-app/nhs-app-features)
- Experiment: The system arranges the sequence of tests and reminders according to the treatment plan. [source](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/06-11-2025-telemedicine-shows-promise-in-improving-dementia-care--who-study-finds)
