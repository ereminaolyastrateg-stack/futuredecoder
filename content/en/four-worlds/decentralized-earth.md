*Order and knowledge emerge through participation, interaction and revisiting experience. The world is an unfolding process in which a person can be a participant and co-author. The task, the method and the meaning of the result are refined together with people, material and environment, and the rules are often worked out by the participants themselves.*

## What it looks like

Neighbours rebuild an old building into a workshop together and agree along the way how to use it. A research group changes its question after every experiment. A teacher sees in class that the plan isn't working and, together with the students, finds another move. There is no ready blueprint: the solution becomes clear in the work, and rules can be revised if experience shows they get in the way.

**Core question:** what emerges and changes in the process of my or our interaction with the world?

## The person of this world

The original map calls them the **"author of one's own life"**. They enter the process with their own experience and change together with what they work on. Being able to take part in setting the task and changing the rules makes their position authorial.

The process can connect a person with another person, a group, a material, a place and the living environment. A partner's response, the resistance of material or a change in conditions become part of the next decision.

## Strength and price

**Strength.** Creating a solution that fits exactly here, noticing new circumstances and developing the ability to act.

**Price.** Participation and agreement take a lot of energy. A shared cause still needs stable agreements, or it falls apart.

## How this world works

| What we look at | How it works |
|---|---|
| Source of order | Interaction of participants, local context, rules worked out and revised |
| Knowledge | Lived experience, tacit skill, trial, observation and reflection on feedback |
| Quality | Fit of the action to the concrete situation; the ability to learn and change approach |
| Role of the person | Author of one's own life, researcher, master, co-author of a shared cause |

The knowledge that arises here can be described and passed on, but it is first obtained only by taking part in what is happening.

## Philosophical foundations

**Michael Polanyi, tacit knowledge:** a person knows more than they can put into an instruction. Practice, attention and participation are part of knowing itself.

**Elinor Ostrom:** a complex shared cause can rest on rules worked out by the participants themselves and on several centres of decision-making.

**Complexity theories:** Cynefin and Ralph Stacey. A solution becomes clear through interaction, conversation, trial and the environment's response. This includes research practice, learning by doing together and revising how work itself is organised.

## Organizational development

- **Greiner — creativity and collaboration.** At one stage a new practice is born; at another, collaboration helps escape excessive formalisation.
- **Adizes:** preserved flexibility and the ability to renew matter. Prime combines flexibility with control, and it can be sustained by combining several worlds.
- **Dynamics:** a local trial gives birth to a new method, which is then described, passed on and fixed. If participants keep the right to change the method and the rules, the search continues.

## In history

- **The local commons** — shared work and agreements around a common resource.
- **Cooperative practices** — participation in work and governance together with discussing the common good and contributions.
- **Modern forms** — communities of practice, joint research, workshops, local and digital commons.

## How our reading is changing

DE used to be associated in our map mainly with local community and relationships between people. Now we look wider: person and group, person and environment, person and material. What matters most is how participation changes understanding, method and the next decisions.

### November 2026: what the lecture "The Future Has Changed Hands" showed

- **Power through micro-communities.** Large systems do not see how small communities organise: a Discord server becomes a temporary parliament, fandom skills become civic ones, a shared anime flag unites people across countries. - **The new elite are makers.** Idols, musicians, artists and local community leaders do not govern; they create images of the future, and makers are the first to set a new vision.
- **The right to be imperfect.** New heroes are not flawless; they have a dark side that is accepted rather than defeated. [[en/materials/signals/2026-nov-dec/sig-2026-039|Signal]]

## Questions of this world

1. Who interacts with whom or with what, and what emerges between them?
2. How does participation change experience, understanding and the quality of the result?
3. Who may revise the rules and the very framing of the question?
4. How does experience become a next step, transferable knowledge and a stable shared practice?
5. How do we stay a circle of our own without becoming a closed club?

## What the games added

In the games a project was described as something that becomes clear in doing it together. Feedback helps change the means and refine the task. Participants also distinguished a mentor's help, the response of those who need the result, and checking its quality.
