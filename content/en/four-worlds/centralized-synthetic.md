*Order through system, standard and reproducibility. The world can be described, divided into operations, measured and organised, and the centre holds the rules. A person enters a ready-made order as a user or executor, and the system itself ensures quality.*

## What it looks like

In the morning the city app builds your route, the insurer recalculates your premium from your watch data, and at work tasks arrive from the system together with instructions. Everything is convenient and predictable: you don't have to invent anything, and you can switch services if one gets boring. But how the rules work is decided not by you but by whoever runs the system. This is the CS world: vast numbers of people and actions coordinated through a shared standard.

**Core question:** what can be measured, standardised and handed to a system to get a predictable result?

## The person of this world

The original map calls them the **"disloyal individualist"**. They look for convenience and their own result and easily switch services, but the way those services work is set by an external system. Their role is user, operator, executor; some of their functions may pass to automation.

## Strength and price

**Strength.** Coordinating huge numbers of actions and providing scale, accounting and repeatability. What works can be applied to millions of cases.

**Price.** The metric starts replacing the substance, and following the procedure replaces solving the problem. It becomes easy to see a person only as a resource.

## How this world works

| What we look at | How it works |
|---|---|
| Source of order | Shared regulations, infrastructure, a model and a governing centre |
| Knowledge | Formal description: categories, indicators, instructions, causal links |
| Quality | Meeting set requirements; reproducibility and controllability |
| Role of the person | User, operator, executor |

"Synthetic" here means an assembled, prepared way of getting a result. It existed long before digital technology — in regulations, on the production line, in accounting systems. A digital platform only amplifies the principle, applying a shared order to ever more cases.

## Philosophical foundations

**Max Weber:** rationalisation and bureaucracy coordinate people through shared rules. **Taylor and Ford:** work can be divided into repeatable operations and assembled into a production system.

**Heidegger, "Enframing":** in the extreme technical view the world becomes a stock of resources. **Habermas, "system":** coordination through power, money and formal mechanisms can crowd out mutual understanding. Together they show both the productive power of CS and the moment when a person becomes only a resource.

In management this logic lives in process approaches, quality standards, ISO, Six Sigma and the formal part of TQM.

## Organizational development

- **Greiner — growth through coordination.** A grown organisation is held together by shared procedures. Too many of them lead to the "red tape" crisis: approvals eat up the ability to act.
- **Adizes — bureaucracy as an extreme form.** The procedure remains while flexibility and the external task are lost. This is a distortion, not a verdict: a working system of standards can be a productive part of an organisation.
- **Mintzberg — machine bureaucracy.** Coordination through standardised processes. CS fixes and scales a found method, while accumulated exceptions create demand for expertise, autonomy or a new search.

## In history

- **Early administrative systems** — accounting, distribution and coordination of many executors; Mumford's "megamachine".
- **Industrial production** — scientific management, the assembly line, the mass standard.
- **Modern infrastructure** — platforms, automated services, scoring, digital public services.

## How our reading is changing

CS used to be recognised in our map mainly by large corporate platforms. Now it also includes government functions moving into ready-made digital services. Within one organisation, CS can sit alongside professional and research processes of other worlds.

## Questions of this world

1. Which properties of a situation can be expressed in a shared model and indicators?
2. Who sets the categories, the standard and the acceptable exceptions?
3. How many different cases can one method serve?
4. Where does measurability start crowding out substance and meaning?

## What the games added

In the games people separated the number of actions, a mastered ability and a useful effect. Measurement can help people grow — or decide who gets access to what. Hence a question for the system itself: who checks its criteria and those who assess?
