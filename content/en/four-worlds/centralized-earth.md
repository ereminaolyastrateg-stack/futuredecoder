*Order through shared meaning, authority and living mastery. The centre sets a goal, a canon or a professional frame, and quality comes from people's participation: understanding the situation, mastering the craft and embodying the shared meaning in concrete work.*

## What it looks like

A person studies for years at a recognised school, works next to a mentor and gradually enters a big shared cause: a research programme, the restoration of a city, a professional community. The goal wasn't set by them, but they understand what they work for and know their work will be judged by people who can do it themselves. This world has its own rituals, language and celebrations. What matters here is not just doing something, but doing it right — the way the school does it.

**Core question:** what do we act together for, what do we consider right, and how do we pass on quality?

## The person of this world

The original map calls them the **"apprentice and bearer of tradition"**. They accept a school or a shared cause and help it continue. Belonging, professional continuity and the ability to embody meaning while keeping it alive matter to them.

## Strength and price

**Strength.** Preserving depth of knowledge, continuity, trust and shared direction. Here people know how to pass on what doesn't fit into an instruction.

**Price.** The canon turns into dogma, and belonging to an institution replaces the quality of work. The school's name starts to weigh more than skill.

## How this world works

| What we look at | How it works |
|---|---|
| Source of order | Recognised authority, a shared goal, a school, tradition or institution |
| Knowledge | Practical wisdom, professional judgement, cultural transmission |
| Quality | Meaningful correspondence to a shared frame, embodied through mastery |
| Role of the person | Participant in a shared cause, apprentice, professional, bearer of tradition |

"Earth" means the result lives in practice and relationships: its quality requires judgement, experience and attention to circumstances. The "Centre" holds the direction and the right of recognition — what counts as good work, who may teach and who can be trusted with a responsible role.

## Philosophical foundations

**Aristotle, practical wisdom:** good action requires the ability to judge a concrete situation. **Gadamer, hermeneutics:** understanding arises in the encounter with a text, an interlocutor and a tradition; a person enters an existing world of meanings and keeps interpreting it.

**Habermas, "lifeworld":** language, norms and cultural experience form the background against which people understand each other.

In methodology this is mentoring, professional schools, working with a canon and mission command: a shared intent combined with decisions on the ground. It keeps the direction when situations differ.

## Organizational development

- **Greiner — growth through direction.** The stage when a shared direction and leadership are established. The crisis of autonomy that follows shows the limit when all decisions are tied to the centre.
- **CE's own cycle:** practice → school → canon. Mastery gets an institution of transmission and recognition — and may then freeze into dogma, when form matters more than living quality.
- **Adizes:** prime requires a balance of control and flexibility. CE helps see the human and cultural side of an institution, and a stage analysis shows whether it can still develop.

## In history

- **School and tradition** — learning around a master and passing on a shared body of knowledge.
- **Monastery, university, guild** — canon, a path of mastery and the right of admission.
- **Professional institution** — a university, lab, company or association around a shared direction and requirements for work.

## How our reading is changing

CE used to be associated in our map primarily with the state. Now we see more clearly companies, labs and professional networks that gather people around a shared vector and long development of mastery. Government functions can combine CE with CS.

## Questions of this world

1. What shared meaning, goal or canon makes our action meaningful?
2. Who recognises quality and the right to pass on knowledge?
3. Which part of mastery is passed on only through participation and relationships?
4. When does loyalty to a school help the cause, and when does it defend dogma?

## What the games added

In the games people distinguished an institution's reputation, demonstrated skill and the right to do a particular job. A mentor can support a learner's own path or prepare them for an assigned role. This clarifies how authority, quality and the right to change direction connect in CE.
