*Independence through an appropriated method, tool and reproducibility. The world is a space of possibilities and tools that a person masters and assembles for their own task. Decisions and control over the method stay with them, and a tuned system helps repeat the result and expand their capabilities.*

## What it looks like

A person has their own toolkit: a personal archive built over years, an AI assistant working on their data, configured automations, several clients instead of one employer. They decide what to take on and how. If a service shuts down, they move their data and keep working. This freedom costs effort: all of it had to be learned and set up, and now it has to be maintained.

**Core question:** what can I master and keep under my control in order to act independently?

## The person of this world

The original map calls them the **"techno-sovereign"**. They try to keep important data, tools and the ability to continue working under any external change. Their role is operator of their own system, independent specialist, entrepreneur.

## Strength and price

**Strength.** Expanding one's capabilities, carrying accumulated work along, recombining tools and offloading repetitive tasks.

**Price.** Autonomy requires infrastructure and maintenance. And external independence can hide a new dependence — on a platform, a model provider, hardware.

## How this world works

| What we look at | How it works |
|---|---|
| Source of order | One's own choice and a managed combination of tools, protocols and agreements |
| Knowledge | A mastered method, a verifiable model, a working template, a portable competence |
| Quality | The chosen method works, the result can be checked and repeated |
| Role of the person | Operator of one's own system, independent specialist, entrepreneur |

"Decentralized" here means control over essential decisions and the ability to continue when external conditions change. One's own system may serve one person or a group of autonomous participants. "Synthetic" lies in the fact that a found method can be repeated and passed on.

## Philosophical foundations

**Technological sovereignty and cypherpunk:** control over data, means of communication and the ability to act should belong to the user — through cryptography, one's own infrastructure and protocols.

**Hayek, dispersed knowledge:** significant information is spread across many people. Hence the question: which decisions should stay with those who have local knowledge and a stake in the result?

In practice this is open source, self-hosting, portable data and digital identity, modular assembly and automating one's own processes. What matters is the move from someone else's service to a method you truly control.

## Organizational development

- **Greiner — growth through delegation.** Units gain independence and responsibility for results. The crisis of control shows that many autonomous loops need coordinating.
- **Adizes — infancy and "Go-Go".** Flexibility and quick action, but often strong dependence on the founder. For DS it matters who actually owns the decisions and the working method.
- **Dynamics:** a found opportunity becomes a mastered tool, and the next question arises — what to maintain yourself, what to combine with others, and which standards are needed for compatibility.

## In history

- **The portable tool** — a way of acting you can learn, take away and apply elsewhere.
- **Personal computing** — computing power passes to the individual, who assembles their own workflows.
- **Open and local systems** — code, data and models on one's own infrastructure, with licences, hardware and support taken into account.

## How our reading is changing

DS used to be recognised in our map by freelancing and the gig economy. Now the focus shifts to **autonomy of intelligence**: who asks the question, owns the accumulated knowledge, chooses the tool and checks the answer. We look at the form of employment together with the real ability to continue working and change the method.

## Questions of this world

1. Which knowledge, data, means and rights stay with the person themselves?
2. How does a found method become a mastered, repeatable tool?
3. What allows a person to act without their former provider or institution?
4. Where does independence require new maintenance, coordination and support?

## What the games added

In the games people separated the efficiency of AI help from independence from its provider. Autonomy was linked to one's own understanding, data, basic skills and resources. And for learning in DS they proposed this: first a practical attempt, then an explanation and a review.
