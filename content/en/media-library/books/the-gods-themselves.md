**Record:** Novel · Isaac Asimov · 1972.

## About the work

Humanity obtains almost inexhaustible energy through an exchange with a parallel universe. The beneficial arrangement conceals dangerous consequences that are difficult to acknowledge once people depend on its advantages. One part of the novel enters the society of beings organised differently from humans.

## Triads

In the parallel world, three distinct beings form a triad. Their roles concern rational understanding, emotional experience and the continuation of life. Each has desires and limitations, while their relationships form a more complex whole.

## Our reading

The triad invites a question: is a contemporary person distributing life among increasingly separate functions — analysis, experience and care for consequences? Someone may calculate a solution well while barely feeling its cost, or experience a situation intensely without knowing how to act.

When some analysis is delegated to AI and different aspects of life are distributed among people and services, the connections between them become especially important. Who brings the whole picture together and accepts responsibility? The triad becomes an image of complementarity: differences expand possibilities when those involved can hear one another and coordinate action.

The energy story reinforces this question. A convenient solution becomes dangerous when its benefits are counted here and now while its consequences fall outside attention. Wholeness requires seeing both the benefit and who will pay its price.

## Questions for discussion

- Where do analysis, experience and care for consequences remain separate in my life?
- What helps bring them together in a decision?
- If I delegate some thinking to AI, what remains my responsibility?
- Which consequences of a beneficial decision do I leave outside attention?
