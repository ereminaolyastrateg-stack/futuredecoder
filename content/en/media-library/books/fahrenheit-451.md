**Record:** Novel · Ray Bradbury · 1953.

## About the work

Guy Montag is a fireman in a society where books are burned. Screen entertainment fills everyday life, until an encounter with someone who asks unfamiliar questions makes him doubt his work and the world around him.

## Why read

The novel explores the disappearance of space for difficult thought. Prohibition is only part of the story: avoiding discomfort, losing time for conversation and replacing judgement with continuous entertainment also matter.

## Education and attention

Learning requires access to books and the ability to stay with a difficult question. When every discomfort is immediately silenced, reading and conversation can begin to feel like unnecessary burdens. How can the desire to understand survive alongside the desire for a quick answer?

## Our reading

Centralized — Synthetic appears in an environment that organises attention and suppresses unwanted ways of thinking; Centralized — Earth appears in treating that arrangement as normal. A personal step begins with a question that no longer fits the available explanation. Knowledge also needs transmission between people: a preserved text becomes alive through reading, conversation and understanding.

## Questions for discussion

- What displaces my attention to difficult things: prohibition, lack of time or a habit of constant distraction?
- Which question do I close too quickly with a ready-made answer?
- How can knowledge be passed on so that its meaning survives, not just its medium?
