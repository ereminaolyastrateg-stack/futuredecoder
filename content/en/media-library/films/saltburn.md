**Record:** Film · Emerald Fennell · 2023.

## About the work

Oxford student Oliver Quick befriends Felix Catton and is invited to spend the summer at his family’s Saltburn estate. Proximity to a privileged world becomes central to a story of desire and belonging.

## Why watch

The film invites us to ask what attracts someone to another life: a person, recognition, possessions, or access to a particular social world.

## In Future Decoder discussions

The film was recommended after viewing and compared with Wuthering Heights, The Menu and Triangle of Sadness. The conversation emphasised uncomfortable relationships and a familiar system in crisis.

## Our reading

Oliver’s path can be read as a move from Centralized — Earth to Centralized — Synthetic: from seeking entry into someone else’s hierarchy and rules of belonging to trying to set conditions, fix roles and control the outcome himself.

The role of women also matters: who grants access, defines the limits of closeness and changes the conditions? Another question concerns the old system’s vulnerability. Possessing a privilege does not ensure its survival when what is already there ceases to be valued.

A newcomer does not necessarily bring renewal. An old order can be destroyed while its dependencies are reproduced and relationships become harsher. The film invites us to ask who is actually ready to offer another way of living together.

## Questions for discussion

- What desire lies behind wanting access to a particular social world?
- How do the female characters set rules and shape change?
- What can a system lose when it stops valuing what it has?
- Why might a replacement for the old order prove worse than its predecessors?
